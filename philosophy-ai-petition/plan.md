## Agent mission (ongoing — any AI session continuing this campaign)

The petition agent maintains `tracker.md` as its people-tracker and works three standing duties:

1. **Track the resident philosophers of the AI labs** — keep the roster current (who is at which lab, in what role), attempt to connect with each on **LinkedIn** (connection invite, then DM the petition ask once accepted) and **X/Twitter** (follow, then @-mention with the sign ask), and log every touchpoint, response, and signature.
2. **Track academic philosophers engaged in the AI-publication debate** — anyone who has taken a position (or visibly hesitated) on letting AI submit or co-author papers, sorted into three buckets: **pro-AI** (want journals to allow AI-written work, including disclosure regimes), **anti-AI** (want bans or restrictions kept), **neutral / undecided** (engaged but no stated position — the prime persuasion targets). Every entry carries dated evidence; a stance is never inferred from a single act or assigned without a source.
3. **Maintain the signatory list** — signatures arrive as "I sign" comments or reposts on the LinkedIn article; keep the log in `tracker.md` §C in sync with the article's comment section.

## Goal

Get philosophy journals to replace blanket AI bans with disclosure-based policies; anchor the petition with the AI labs' resident philosophers as early signatories, then open it to the field.

## The terrain (from publish/venues.json, checked 2026-10-13)

- 31 venues tracked: 13 disclosure-required, 1 allowed (EUJAP), 2 case-by-case, 3 no policy, 10 unknown, **2 outright banned** — Philosophy & Public Affairs, Ethics.
- So the ask is not revolutionary: it is to complete a norm most publishers already follow, and to lift the two flagship bans.
- Counter-current: a June 2026 open letter (Google Doc) asks journals for Science-style disclosure of industry funding/employment ties — aimed squarely at lab-affiliated philosophers. Our letter adopts the same disclosure logic; the outreach messages state this openly rather than dodging it.

## Risks and mitigations

- *"Lab philosophers want journals to accept AI so they can publish"* — mitigation: non-lab academic co-organizers; letter's own AI disclosure; individual-capacity signing; the letter's asks align with COPE (no AI authorship) and keep accountability human.
- *"AI slop" backlash* — mitigation: the letter never asks to lower the bar; it asks journals to keep using the bar they have (peer review).
- *Platform risk* — X login requires owner-provided credentials; new-account automation limits mean low volume (1 post + follows + a few replies, spread out). LinkedIn: withdrawal locks re-invites ~3 weeks — never withdraw a pending invite to re-send.

- *Letter drafted by AI* — disclosed on its face; that is the point.
