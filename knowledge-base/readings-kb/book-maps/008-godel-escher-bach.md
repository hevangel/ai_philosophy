## Part I: GEB

- **Introduction: A Musico-Logical Offering** [in ch17 stratum / Overview, O]. Bach's *Musical Offering* as the book's theme: self-reference and interplay of levels in Bach, Escher, and Gödel; a brief history of logic and paradox; mechanical reasoning and the AI question.
- **Ch. I: The MU-puzzle** ★ [ch18, R] → *Gödelian Self-Reference and the Limits of Formal Systems*; *Arguments, Validity, and Soundness* (background). The MIU-system (Post production system): strings, the four rules, the **Requirement of Formality**; **theorems** as strings *produced* by rules (vs Theorems, statements *proved*); axioms, rules of production/inference, derivations; working **inside vs outside the system** (M-mode / I-mode / U-mode); **decision procedures** — a test for theoremhood guaranteed to terminate; axioms, unlike theorems, must have one. The chapter file ends with Carroll's *Two-Part Invention* ("What the Tortoise Said to Achilles"): the infinite regress of having to add each rule of inference as a further premise.
- **Ch. II: Meaning and Form in Mathematics** ★ [ch19, R in key sections] → *Gödelian Self-Reference…*; *Semantic Analysis and the Clarity of Meaning* (connection). The pq-system: apparently meaningless symbols acquire meaning when an **isomorphism** (information-preserving mapping) between theorems and true additions is perceived; meaning in a formal system is *passive* — it licenses reading strings, never creating new theorems beyond the rules (the Requirement of Formality again). *Sonata for Unaccompanied Achilles* (figure/ground).
- **Ch. III: Figure and Ground** [ch20, O]. Figure/ground in art vs theorems/nontheorems; recursively enumerable vs recursive sets. *Contracrostipunctus* — the dialogue carrying paraphrases of Gödel's construction ("for each record player, a record it cannot play").
- **Ch. IV: Consistency, Completeness, and Geometry** [ch21, O + opening]. How symbols acquire meaning; Euclidean vs non-Euclidean geometry and "undefined terms"; consistency of rival geometries. *Little Harmonic Labyrinth* (nested stories).
- **Ch. V: Recursive Structures and Processes** [inside ch21, O]. Recursion across music, language, geometry, mathematics, programs. *Canon by Intervallic Augmentation*.
- **Ch. VI: The Location of Meaning** [ch22, O]. Meaning divided among coded message, decoder, receiver: DNA, undeciphered inscriptions, records in space; is meaning inherent or manufactured by the interpreting mechanism?
- **Ch. VII: The Propositional Calculus** ★ [ch23, R in key sections] → *Arguments, Validity, and Soundness*. A formal system for "and," negation, implication: formation rules for well-formed strings; Rule of Joining, Rule of Separation, Double-Tilde Rule; the **Fantasy Rule** (assume x, derive y, conclude <x⊃y>) and the Carry-Over Rule — the system's machinery doing what Achilles could not make the word "and" do for the Tortoise. Examples are Zen sentences ("Zentences").
- **Ch. VIII: Typographical Number Theory (TNT)** [ch24, O + opening]. The Propositional Calculus extended to number theory: rigid symbol manipulation for arithmetical reasoning; Gödel-numbering prepared; indirect self-reference in the *Crab Canon*.
- **Ch. IX: Mumon and Gödel** [ch25, O]. Zen and the philosophy of mathematics; **Gödel-numbering** introduced and a first pass through the Incompleteness Theorem.
- **Ch. X: Levels of Description, and Computer Systems** [ch26, O]. Gödel's string G and a Bach fugue as things understandable on several levels; hardware/software, ants and ant colonies. *Ant Fugue* (holism vs reductionism).

## Part II: EGB

- **Ch. XI: Brains and Thoughts** [inside ch27, O]. Brains as symbol systems; active subsystems ("symbols") as the level at which minds might be mapped. *English French German Suite*.
- **Ch. XII: Minds and Thoughts** [ch28, O]. Isomorphism (or partial isomorphism) between two brains at the symbol level.
- **Ch. XIII: BlooP and FlooP and GlooP** [ch29, O]. Three toy computer languages for primitive vs general recursivity — the machinery that will make "proof-pair" a testable property in Ch. XIV. *Air on G's String* (quining: how a sentence can reproduce itself).
- **Ch. XIV: On Formally Undecidable Propositions of TNT and Related Systems** ★ [ch30, R] → *Gödelian Self-Reference and the Limits of Formal Systems*. The proof in intuitive form, built from two ideas: **Gödel-numbering** (TNT strings can speak about TNT strings — introspection via proof-pairs, a primitive recursive and hence TNT-representable property) and the **diagonal focusing trick** ("arithmoquining": substituting a formula's own Gödel number into itself). Result: the string G whose interpretation is "G is not a theorem of TNT"; neither G nor ~G is a theorem (if TNT is consistent); **Gödel's Second Theorem** — "TNT is consistent" is expressible in TNT but provable there only if TNT is inconsistent; TNT is ω-incomplete; the two extensions (add G, or add ~G with its "supernatural numbers") and non-standard number theory; Hilbert's Tenth Problem.
- **Ch. XV: Jumping out of the System** ★ [ch31, R in main sections] → *Gödelian Self-Reference…*. The construction reapplied to every extension (TNT+G, axiom schemas): **essential incompleteness**; the three conditions under which any consistent system is vulnerable (expressiveness, proof-pair representability, self-reference capacity — the "critical mass" passage); **Lucas's argument** that Gödel's theorem refutes mechanism and Hofstadter's rebuttal begun (the "Gödelizing operator," Escher's *Dragon*, the limits of intelligent systems — we too cannot Gödelize arbitrarily complex systems on demand).
- **Ch. XVI: Self-Ref and Self-Rep** [ch32, O]. Mechanisms of self-reference in sentences, and of self-reproduction in DNA and machines, compared.
- **Ch. XVII: Church, Turing, Tarski, and Others** [ch33, O]. The book's thesis stated: every aspect of thinking as a high-level description of a system governed at low level by simple, even formal, rules; computability and the limits of formalisation.
- **Ch. XVIII: Artificial Intelligence: Retrospects** [ch34, O]. Turing's 1950 article and the first decades of AI.
- **Ch. XIX: Artificial Intelligence: Prospects** [ch35, duplicated at ch44; O]. Counterfactuals and "almost" situations; how thought lets some features "slip" — the flexibility formal systems lack.
- **Ch. XX: Strange Loops, or Tangled Hierarchies** [ch36, O]. The closing chapter: Strange Loops, tangled hierarchies, and whether machines can possess originality.
