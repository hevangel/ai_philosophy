What follows is a map of Dolphin's book on its own terms — it is a work of geoscience *education research* that uses history and philosophy of science as teaching material, which is the only sense in which it belongs anywhere near this slice.

- **Front Matter** [file ch01]. Series page for *Science: Philosophy, History and Education* (a series devoted to leveraging history and philosophy of science to improve science education), title page, and the book's opening apparatus. Author: Glenn Dolphin.
- **Part I — Designing the Course with Teaching the Nature of Science in Mind** [divider + introduction, file ch02]. Frames the book's project: an introductory geology course for non-science majors (200–300 students) redesigned as an exercise in instructional "design engineering" — understand the problem, delineate constraints, implement strategies, measure outcomes, iterate — with geoscience literacy as the public-facing rationale.
- **1. Earth Science Education: Rationales and Current State** [file ch03]. Why geoscience matters (the earth system, natural hazards, resources, soils, water) and why it is neglected in science literacy. The sections nearest to this KB's concerns are §1.3, on the role of history and the nature of science (NOS) in geoscience education — characterising NOS for science education, the "family resemblance" approach, "questions rather than tenets" — and §1.4, on using *history* to teach NOS and on helping students "think like a geologist", with case studies as the vehicle. §1.5 lays out the book's structure. This is philosophy of science at one remove: NOS scholarship summarised for course design, not argued.
- **2. Transformative Teaching: From Educator to Design Engineer** [file ch04]. The design-engineering model of teaching: surveying the educational landscape and its constraints, interactivity and interpretation, ideation and prototyping of strategies, classroom experiments. Education theory and course logistics; no philosophy-of-history content.
- **3. Biographies of Three Different Concepts** [file ch05]. The book's substantive core, and the only chapter with extended history-of-science narrative. Dolphin teaches the course as three braided *historical narratives* ("biographies" of concepts), each framed by an essential question: (I) *the earth's biography* — "How can we understand the historical nature of the earth?" (§3.2: Steno reading the "signs" in the rocks — superposition, original horizontality, lateral continuity, cross-cutting — and reasoning out an earth history; Hutton's eternal, cyclical earth and uniformitarianism, "the present is the key to the past"; fossils given historical meaning through Anning, Cuvier's fixity of kinds against Lamarck, and Darwin's 1859 synthesis; fossil-based mapping; ice ages — the earth emerges as *historical and contingent*, against Hutton's eternalism); (II) *the age of the earth* (§3.3: bottom-up empiricism, Kelvin-era thermodynamics as a "top-down" approach, and radioactivity transforming the dating game — the discovery of deep time); (III) *developing a theory of the earth* — "Why are there still mountains?" (§3.4: contractionism and permanentism, drifters vs fixists, and the geophysics — seismology, seafloor exploration, geomagnetism — that assembled plate tectonics). §3.1 explains the narrative pedagogy: historical interludes present observations *and competing interpretations* so students decide without hindsight, then inquiry and model-based learning iterate toward the current consensus. This chapter is a competent *retelling* of the history of geology; it advances no philosophical theses of its own about historical explanation, narrative, or objectivity, and cites the history through secondary sources (Rudwick, Repcheck, Cutler).
- **4. Transformative Teaching Strategies** [file ch06]. The course's methods in detail: team-based learning as foundation, case/problem-based learning, inquiry activities (visible geology, seismic, seafloor data, age of the earth, earth dynamics), model-based learning, a Wikipedia project highlighting women of geology, and explicit reflective NOS instruction. Of peripheral note for this KB: §4.8.2, "Geology as a Historical and Hermeneutic Science", §4.8.3 on resolving controversy in geology, §4.8.5 on serendipity, and §4.8.6 on prematurity in geologic discovery — the book's closest approach to philosophy of a historical science, in a few pages of teaching commentary.
- **Part II — Measuring Shifts in Students' Content Knowledge and Nature of Science Understandings** [divider + introduction, file ch07]. Introduces the assessment programme: click-on-diagram questions via a classroom response system, the Geoscience Concept Inventory (GCI), and the VOSTS survey, administered pre/post.
- **5. Measuring Student Learning** [file ch08]. Methods and results for the three instruments of Part II, with discussion. Empirical education research.
- **6. Students' Understandings of Science and Scientists** [file ch09]. Qualitative study of how students' views shifted over the course: from "science is situational" to doing science; from routine to creative; scientific knowledge "from an always changing truth to grades of reliability"; scientists "from an idealized to humanized" picture. The chapter in which the extracted book comes closest to epistemological *findings*, though its subject is students' beliefs, not the justification of historical knowledge itself.
- **7. Students' Metaphors for Science** [file ch10]. Conceptual-metaphor analysis of student language: science knowledge as moving along a path, as an edifice, as a landscape, as liquid/ocean, as a plant; scientific theory as a puzzle; ideas as objects. Discussion of how such metaphors frame science processes and products.
- **8. Concluding Remarks** [file ch11]. Recapitulation: the importance of geoscience education; design engineering and reflective practice; the three narratives; the strategies; the learning measurements; final words on results and on process.
- **Back Matter** [file ch12]. The book's index only (case studies → Wikipedia). 158 words of index entries; no bibliography file is present in the extraction.
