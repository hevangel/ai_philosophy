# Book map — 005: Gary L. Hardcastle & George A. Reisch (eds.), *Bullshit and Philosophy: Guaranteed to Get Perfect Results Every Time* (Open Court, 2006)

Every Frankfurt claim in this slice's entry is therefore Frankfurt *as presented by these essays*, and is labelled as such.

## Introduction
- **On Bullshitmania** — George A. Reisch & Gary L. Hardcastle [file ch05]. Why a tiny 1986 essay became a 2005 bestseller: an Iraq-war-era culture of spin, fabricated memoirs (Frey, "J.T. Leroy"), blurred advertising, and scientific fraud made Frankfurt's warning — bullshit as indifference to truth, corrosive of the true/false distinction itself — feel prophetic. Philosophy's own anti-bullshit lineage: Socrates, Descartes, and Hume's "commit it then to the flames" as earlier purges of sophistry and illusion.

## Part I — To Shoot the Bull? (Rethinking and Responding to Bullshit)
- **1. Scott Kimbrough — On Letting It Slide** [file ch07, "Bullshit and Truth"] ★. Frankfurt's puzzle — why are we *more* tolerant of bullshit than of lying, if bullshit is the greater enemy of truth? — answered socially: politeness and the faked apology (via William Ian Miller's *Faking It*) sacrifice truth for peace, so on Frankfurt's definition "bullshit constitutes the greater part of civility"; entertainment value; and respect for the ends bullshitters serve (selling, recruiting, fund-raising), with contempt reserved for the "suckers" who fall for it. Intolerable bullshit: bad reasons given for decisions that affect us (the passed-over promotion; Orwell on political language). **Bullshit and self-deception**: bullshit can be produced *unintentionally* — the self-deceived talk-radio fan — contra Frankfurt's intentionalism. **Blameless bullshit**: Newton's alchemy is bullshit now, though Newton is no bullshitter; the charge's function is to license dismissal, and its real target is *method* — bullshit results from "lame methods of justification," adopted intentionally, self-deceptively, or blamelessly. Conclusion: bullshit cannot be eradicated, only recognised and "restricted to truly justifiable uses"; wholesale indifference to truth is never justifiable, since even effective bullshitting presupposes a lively concern for truth.
- **2. Consuelo Preti — A Defense of Common Sense** [file ch08] ★ (supporting). Both Frankfurt and Cohen omit the third element: the *audience*. Bullshit needs our compliance — it sells us significance and flatters our desire to feel important; academic bullshit (including bullshit in academic decision-making) works the same way. Resistance is therefore partly ours to supply: G. E. Moore as the model "resolute carer" about truth — the method of "What *exactly* do you mean?" Bullshit's paradox: it must make itself matter to us, so if what matters to us is the true/false distinction, bullshit is put "on a short leash."
- **3. George A. Reisch — The Pragmatics of Bullshit, Intelligently Designed** [file ch09] ★. A rival definition. Frankfurt's puzzle is an artefact of *semantics*: bullshitters do not sin against truth at all — they sin against *pragmatics*, cloaking one engagement inside another. The case study is Intelligent Design: its advocates are not indifferent to truth (they care intensely about their own truths, about culture and "modernism"); what they conceal is an *agenda* — an evangelical programme inside a science-education programme. Bullshit = the orchestration of two concerns, one used as cover for the other; hence we tolerate it (lies thwart needs we already have; bullshit tries to *create* needs, and fails without a gullible audience; its dupes are often sincere). **Purism**: properly defined, there is much less bullshit than casual usage suggests — bad news and false claims are not bullshit unless the two-engagement structure is present.
- **4. Kenneth A. Taylor — Bullshit and the Foibles of the Human Mind, or: What the Masters of the Dark Arts Know** [file ch10]. Public discourse is debased by bullshit, propaganda, spin, and lies — but purveyors "cannot sell what we do not buy": the fault lies partly in enduring cognitive foibles (framing effects and their kin), now expertly exploited, with vastly greater reach. Remedies on two fronts: education (distaste for dogma, re-framing skills, appetite for disquieting evidence) and reconfiguring privileged access to the means of persuasion.
- **5. Sara Bernal — Bullshit and Personality** [file ch11] ★. Against Frankfurt: the typical bullshitter is *not* indifferent to truth but is strongly connected to it "via a desire to obscure a specific part of it" — her **target**, which she may know clearly, dimly, or not at all (unconscious motives; split-brain confabulation as evidence such motives operate). Two modes: (a) *indirect implication of falsehood* (Newport's "alive with pleasure") and (b) *distraction* (the Niger yellowcake story). Unlike the liar she never denies the target directly; conscious bullshitting differs from lying only in method, unconscious bullshitting in more. Applied to the DSM-IV personality disorders, whose distorted social perception supplies easy targets and whose bullshitting helps ruin their relationships. The threat: because bullshitting can be unconscious, it provokes less conscience than lying and is therefore easier to make a habit of.
- **6. Alan Richardson — Performing Bullshit and the Post-Sincere Condition** [file ch12]. Opens with a parody mission statement, its subject. Frankfurt's Pascal/Wittgenstein anecdote turned back on his account ("shitty attitudes"); bullshit as performance and as a standing condition of institutional life (the corporate mission statement) once sincerity is drained out — "the world as will to bullshit." Remedies are thin: the Jon Stewart gambit (openly made-up news that reveals truth better than "serious" sources — humour as "the sincerest form of unconcern for the truth"), or the hard work of writing institutional statements one actually means.
- **7. Cornelis de Waal — The Importance of Being Earnest: A Pragmatic Approach to Bullshitting** [file ch13] ★ (supporting). Names the volume's two schools: Cohen's *structuralist* (bullshit as product) vs Frankfurt's *intentionalist* (bullshit as process). De Waal's own frame: bullshitting as failed or fake *inquiry* — passing claims off as the product of inquiry that was never honestly undertaken — governed by an **epistemic imperative**. Hence some bullshitting is permissible: where a competing purpose (easing an interaction — "It's nice to see you!") rightly overrules the imperative, or where conclusions don't matter and the enjoyment of the game does.

## Part II — The Bull by the Horns (Defining Bullshit)
- **8. G. A. Cohen — Deeper into Bullshit** [file ch15] ★. The volume's philosophical centre of gravity. The OED already distinguishes two senses: bullshit as *insincere talk* (Frankfurt's — the "bull" wears the trousers: bullshit is whatever a bullshitter produces) and bullshit as *nonsense/rubbish* (Cohen's — the "shit" wears the trousers: a property of the output, whatever the producer's state of mind). Frankfurt's "essence" claim overreaches: he characterises one kind only. Even for that kind, his lying/bullshitting contrast is malconstructed — he identifies the liar by his *goal* (misleading about reality) but the bullshitter by his *tactic* (asserting without caring), so paradigm bullshitters (advertisers) turn out, on his account, to be lying whenever they mislead. Cohen's own kind: **unclarifiable unclarity** — discourse that cannot be rendered clear in anything recognisable as a version of what was said (test: adding or subtracting a negation changes nothing about its plausibility), minus the saving virtue of suggestiveness that acquits good poetry. Bullshit as *product*, not process: polemic should target the visible product, not the invisible process — an honest person can utter bullshit; a Frankfurt-bullshitter can accidentally talk sense.
- **9. Gary L. Hardcastle — The Unity of Bullshit** [file ch16]. Is there one phenomenon here? Via Carnap and the Vienna Circle (bullshit as what logical analysis eliminates) and Laura Penny: bullshit is one part *tool* (language amenable to misuse) and one part *intention* (to put something over by means of it). Since intentions cannot be eradicated, control the tool: the language is held in common, and each speaker bears responsibility for its misuse "in our presence."
- **10. Andrew Aberdein — Raising the Tone: Definition, Bullshit, and the Definition of Bullshit** [file ch17] ★ (supporting). Frankfurt's innovation recast as naming a linguistic misbehaviour, **semantic negligence** (against the liar's semantic *deception*). Through Frege on sense and reference to *persuasive definition* (PD) — redefining a loaded term while trading on its tone. Verdict: Frankfurt's definition *is* PD (it changes the sense, hence the reference, of 'bullshit' — kindly "help is on its way" cases fall inside it; Cohen's cases fall outside), but PD is malign only when semantically negligent; Frankfurt argues explicitly for his departures from usage, so the definition is PD but diligent — though Cohen's omissions remain "harder to defend."
- **11. Hans Maes & Katrien Schaubroeck — Different Kinds and Aspects of Bullshit** [file ch18] ★. The volume's best single survey of Frankfurt: two essential ingredients — indifference to truth *and* pretence (the bullshitter misrepresents his *enterprise*, not the facts; hence bull sessions, which lack pretence, are excluded by Frankfurt) — and the conclusion that bullshit is "a greater enemy of the truth than lies are." Two revisions: (i) **pretence is not essential** — Fania Pascal's "I feel just like a dog that has been run over" is bullshit (for Wittgenstein) with no deceit in sight, and an openly truth-indifferent politician would still be bullshitting; Frankfurt's bullshit/bull-session distinction is artificial. (ii) **bullshit is not always bad** — banter, wit, and polite formulae (Nagel: "How nice to see you" deceives no one, because the convention is common knowledge) are benign and socially load-bearing; this, not a mystery, explains our benign attitude to much bullshit. Then a **third kind** that neither Frankfurt nor Cohen covers: *pseudoscience* — its producers are typically sincere truth-seekers (not Frankfurt-indifferent) whose claims are often perfectly clear (not Cohen-unclarifiable); its marks are insensitivity to evidence and fallacious reasoning.

## Part III — It's All Around Us (Bullshit in Politics, Science, Education, and the Law)
- **12. Mark Evans — The Republic of Bullshit: On the Dumbing-Up of Democracy** [file ch20]. The **Frankfurt thesis**: democracy itself *requires* citizens to hold and voice opinions on everything, hence structurally encourages bullshitting ("unavoidable whenever circumstances require someone to talk without knowing what he is talking about"). Weighed against a "cynicism thesis"; both partly right. Citizens' own bullshit (not only politicians') degrades political perception; philosophy can inoculate only if it is the truth-aiming kind — fashionable truth-disparaging philosophy is itself part of the clog.
- **13. Vanessa Neumann — Political Bullshit and the Stoic Story of Self** [file ch21]. A third context beyond Frankfurt's utterances and Cohen's texts: the *mechanism* of bullshit. Political bullshit works by supplying **stories** that shape feelings and confer identity ("spreading democracy"), bypassing the faculties of reason and debate; its pull is that it makes audiences players in a narrative. Counter-strategy borrowed from Cohen: identify the content in order to eradicate the process.
- **14. Heather Douglas — Bullshit at the Interface of Science and Policy** [file ch22]. Two kinds at the science–policy interface: bullshit of the **isolated fact** (cherry-picking one fact or study — climate records — while ignoring the body of evidence) and bullshit of **universal standards** (dismissing good science for failing an impossible standard of proof). The second can be permanently undermined by dropping the fiction of universal standards and arguing instead about strength of evidence and risks of error; the first persists wherever dishonest operators find an audience.
- **15. David J. Tietge — Rhetoric Is Not Bullshit** [file ch23]. 'Rhetoric' has been unfairly collapsed into 'bullshit.' Rhetoric *utens* vs rhetoric as methods for analysing texts; postmodernism's bad reputation examined. The vast grey area between Truth and bullshit is rhetoric's domain; rhetorical education (real rhetorical situations, commonplace books) is the citizen's navigation skill for it.
- **16. Steve Fuller — Just Bullshit** [file ch24]. The dissenting chapter. The bullshit-detector's stance converts an epistemic attitude into a moral virtue ("reality can be known only by the right sort of person"); bullshit, charitably read, is sophistry — an acknowledgement that truth alone may not defeat an artfully presented claim; its closest theological kin is hypocrisy, which makes bullshitters cognitive *equals* of liars, not truth-indifferents. Bullshit as a call to open-mindedness and as "deferred epistemic gratification"; Wittgenstein as detector-or-bullshitter; closing on science in the courtroom (*McLean v. Arkansas* praised for a neutral standard; *Kitzmiller* criticised for a "customized" one) — justice as cutting through the bullshit on *both* sides.
