**Extraction note — the whole story.** The folder (`data/philosophy_readings/markdown/004 - On Manners/`) was catalogued under the title *On Manners*, presumably on the strength of the author's surname. Its title page (file ch02) reads: **THE PANNELL WITCH — BASED ON A TRUE STORY — MELISSA MANNERS — MELISSA MANNERS PUBLISHING**. The copyright page (file ch04) gives © 2022 Melissa Manners, ISBN 978-1-7396451-2-0 (eBook) / 978-1-7396451-3-7 (paperback). It is a self-published work of historical fiction about Mary Pannell, a Yorkshire healer tried for witchcraft — not a work of philosophy, and not about manners, etiquette, or politeness in any sense. **There is no philosophy text in this folder at all**: a full-text search finds "philosoph-" exactly three times in ~72,000 words, each an incidental period reference (e.g. the "School of Night" chapter), and no discussion of ethics, etiquette, or moral theory anywhere. The extraction itself is complete and clean — the mislabelling happened at acquisition/cataloguing, not in the splitting.

Every one of the 51 files was inspected for this map. Structure: front matter (files ch01–ch05: cover stub, title page, contents, copyright, dedication stub), 42 narrative chapters (files ch06–ch47), and back matter (files ch48–ch51). The chapters rotate among three first-person/third-person viewpoints — Elizabeth, Mary, Bess (the Pannell daughters and their mother) — across two timelines (Kippax and London, 1593–1603, with the prequel reaching back to 1556). Chapter titles below are the book's own, with narrator and extraction file; one line each, from each chapter's opening, is all a novel needs for audit purposes — and all it can honestly be given in a philosophy book layer.

## Back matter
- **Connect With Me!** [ch48]. The author's request for reviews, as an indie author.
- **Epilogue** [ch49]. The historical basis: Mary Pannell (née Tailor) of Kippax, West Yorkshire, married John Pannell in 1559; biographical notes on the real Mary "Molly" Witham (later Dame Mary Bolles, baronetess) and George Chapman. Fiction with roots in reality, by the author's own account.
- **About the Author** [ch50]. Melissa Manners, London-born; writes historical fiction to reframe narratives "passed down to us, mostly by men" and to give neglected historical figures a voice; the English witch trials and the treatment of women are her stated subject.
- **Becoming The Pannell Witch: A Prequel** [ch51]. Opening chapter of the prequel (Kippax, 1556): young Mary, carrots in hand, rehearsing what to say to her father.
