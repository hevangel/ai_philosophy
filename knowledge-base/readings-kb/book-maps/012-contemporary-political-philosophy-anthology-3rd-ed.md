# Book map — 012 (first folder): Robert E. Goodin and Philip Pettit (eds.), *Contemporary Political Philosophy: An Anthology*, 3rd edition (Wiley-Blackwell, 2019)

*Book layer · Political philosophy. ★ = chapter feeds one of this slice's problem entries.*

**Identity note — read this first.** The task brief for this slice described this folder
(`data/philosophy_readings/markdown/012 - Contemporary Political Philosophy/`) as
"Kymlicka's introduction" (i.e. Will Kymlicka, *Contemporary Political Philosophy: An
Introduction*). **The files do not bear that out.** The folder's own copyright page
(file ch05) states: "The right of Robert E. Goodin and Philip Pettit to be identified
as the authors of the editorial material in this work has been asserted"; the series
page (file ch03) lists "Goodin and Pettit: *Contemporary Political Philosophy: An
Anthology* (third edition)"; the Preface (file ch06) is signed Robert E. Goodin and
Philip Pettit. This folder is the **3rd edition (2019) of the Goodin & Pettit
anthology**, with full text. No copy of Kymlicka's *Introduction* exists in the
readings corpus; Kymlicka appears in this slice only as a contributor (his
"Justice and Minority Rights" is ch. 25 of the 2nd-edition anthology — a stub in the
reconstruction folder, and ch. 25's counterpart "Justice and Minority Rights" material
appears in this 3rd edition at file ch36). This map was first delivered under a "kymlicka" filename inherited from the build brief; it has since been renamed to record the book the folder actually contains. The companion map
`012-contemporary-political-philosophy-anthology-2nd-ed.md` covers the *second* folder,
which is a reconstruction of the **2nd edition** of the same anthology — the two 012s
are two editions of one anthology, not two different books, and they are kept distinct
throughout this slice.

**Extraction note.** 66 files. Front matter: ch01 (cover, empty), ch02 (table of
contents — the reliable chapter list used below), ch03–ch05 (series page, title page,
copyright), ch06 (editors' Preface). Body text runs ch08–ch64 in book order, with
one-file part dividers (ch07, ch12, ch18, ch24, ch30, ch37, ch43, ch49, ch56) and
ch65 (index), ch66 (licence). File titles are epub-spine labels and are **unreliable**:
several files are titled "References" but contain body text of the following chapter,
and most chapters are split across two consecutive files (text file + continuation).
The map below therefore gives the book's own numbering from the extracted TOC, with
file numbers verified at the chapter head where inspected; treat other file
attributions as sequential approximations. Full text throughout — no stubs found in
this folder.

- **Preface** [ch06]. The essays are "canonical texts in contemporary political philosophy" but only a sample; the collection is a companion to the editors' *Companion to Contemporary Political Philosophy*, designed to show political philosophy "as a set of ongoing conversations and disputations, arguments, and debates."

## Part I — Doing Political Philosophy (chs. 1–4)
- **1. Realism and Moralism in Political Theory** — Bernard Williams [ch08]. Enactment vs political-realist models of theory; the first political question and the Basic Legitimation Demand.
- **2. What Do We Want from a Theory of Justice?** — Amartya Sen [ch09]. Transcendental institutionalism vs the comparative approach to justice.
- **3. Utopophobia** — David Estlund (per TOC sections; file ch10). Defends aspirational/ideal theory against feasibility objections.
- **4. *Political* Political Theory** — Jeremy Waldron [ch11]. Hume on persons vs institutions; realism vs moralism; the dominance of justice as a topic.

## Part II — State and Society (chs. 5–9)
- **5. The State** — Quentin Skinner [text begins ch13]. The modern concept of the state, from Hobbes onward.
- **6. State Simplification** — James C. Scott [ch14]. Legibility: how states simplify social facts (the scientific-forestry parable) to govern.
- **7. The Liberal State** [ch15]. (Author not verified from the extraction head.)
- **8. The Market and the Forum** — Jon Elster [ch16]. Three views of politics; social choice theory treated as an instrumental, aggregative model — background to the Arrow entry.
- **9. Invoking Civil Society** — Charles Taylor [ch17]. The revived concept of civil society against the state.

## Part III — Democracy (chs. 10–14)
- **10. The Public Sphere** — Jürgen Habermas [ch19]. Concept and history of the public sphere; its form in mass welfare-state democracies.
- **11. Procedural Democracy** — Robert A. Dahl [ch20] ★ → *Democracy and the Voting Paradoxes*. Criteria a procedure must meet to be fully democratic, derived from minimal assumptions about an association; closes on the problem of inclusion — who counts as a citizen entitled to participate.
- **12. Deliberation and Democratic Legitimacy** — Joshua Cohen [ch21]. Deliberative democracy as a fundamental, not derivative, ideal.
- **13. Dealing with Difference** — Anne Phillips [ch22]. Politics of ideas vs politics of presence.
- **14. A "Selection Model" of Political Representation** [ch23]. Sanctions vs selection models of the representative relation.

## Part IV — Justice (chs. 15–19)
- **15. Justice as Fairness** — John Rawls [ch25] ★ → *Distributive Justice*. The 1958 paper (not *A Theory of Justice*): justice as a virtue of practices; two principles — equal right to the most extensive liberty compatible with a like liberty for all, and inequalities arbitrary unless reasonably expected to work out for everyone's advantage and attached to positions open to all.
- **16. Nozick's Entitlements** — Onora O'Neill [ch26] ★ → *Distributive Justice*. A critical staging of Nozick: patterned vs end-result vs entitlement theories; argues the Wilt Chamberlain argument presupposes the full capitalist property rights it is meant to establish; presses the missing principle of just acquisition.
- **17. Justice Engendered** — Martha Minow [ch27]. The dilemma of difference in law and policy.
- **18. Political Responsibility and Structural Injustice** — Iris Marion Young [ch28]. Structural injustice and forward-looking political responsibility (the Sandy housing case).
- **19. Superseding Historic Injustice** — Jeremy Waldron [ch29]. Reparation, counterfactual approaches, and when changed circumstances supersede historic claims.

## Part V — Rights (chs. 20–25)
- **20. Are There Any Natural Rights?** — H. L. A. Hart [ch31]. Argues that if there are any moral rights there is at least one natural right: the equal right of all men to be free.
- **21. Taking Rights Seriously** — Ronald Dworkin [ch32]. Rights as trumps; rights of citizens, controversial rights, and why take rights seriously.
- **22. Basic Rights** — Henry Shue [ch33]. Subsistence and security rights; the tripartite duties (avoid, protect, aid) and their interdependence.
- **23. The Dark Side of Human Rights** [ch34]. Norms, aspirations, cynicism; state obligations, control and blame.
- **24. A Defense of Abortion** — Judith Jarvis Thomson [ch35]. Grants the fetus personhood for argument's sake and denies the conclusion follows. (Same paper is the one full-text item besides Berlin in the 2nd-edition reconstruction folder.)
- **25. Justice and Minority Rights** — Will Kymlicka [ch36]. Three forms of group-differentiated rights; the equality argument; historical agreements; cultural diversity.

## Part VI — Liberty (chs. 26–30)
- **26. Two Concepts of Liberty** — Isaiah Berlin [ch38]. Negative vs positive freedom; the retreat to the inner citadel; the one and the many. (Full text also in the 2nd-edition reconstruction folder, ch24 there.)
- **27. What's Wrong with Negative Liberty?** — Charles Taylor [ch39]. Exercise vs opportunity concepts of freedom.
- **28. A Third Concept of Liberty** — Quentin Skinner [ch40]. Freedom as non-dependence, against the MacCallum one-formula view.
- **29. Libertarian Paternalism** — Thaler & Sunstein [ch41]. Choice architecture; whether paternalism is inevitable.
- **30. Political Liberalism and Religion** — Cécile Laborde [ch42]. Public reason, separation and establishment.

## Part VII — Equality (chs. 31–35)
- **31. Complex Equality** — Michael Walzer [ch44]. Spheres of goods; dominance and monopoly; simple vs complex equality.
- **32. Equality of What?** — Amartya Sen [ch45]. Utilitarian, total-utility, and Rawlsian equality all found limited; basic capability equality proposed.
- **33. Equality and Priority** [ch46]. Egalitarianism vs the priority view (Nagel's two-children case appears in the extracted text).
- **34. Chance, Choice, and Justice** [ch47]. Compensation and reward principles; choicism and its qualifications (luck egalitarianism's core chapter).
- **35. What Is the Point of Equality?** — Elizabeth Anderson [ch48]. Against luck egalitarianism ("equality of fortune"); democratic equality as relating as equals.

## Part VIII — Oppression (chs. 36–41)
- **36. Power, Right, Truth** — Michel Foucault [ch50]. The *how* of power, between rules of right and effects of truth.
- **37. The Domination Contract** — Charles Mills [ch51]. The social contract as a domination contract (companion to Pateman).
- **38. Race, Sex, and Indifference** [ch52]. Race and reproduction; miscegenation law (the Alabama amendment case opens the extracted text).
- **39. Respecting Beliefs and Rebuking Rushdie** — Peter Jones [ch53]. How far a plural society must defer to beliefs it does not share.
- **40. The Structure of Proletarian Unfreedom** — G. A. Cohen [ch54]. Marx's proletarian defined by having only labour power to sell; collective unfreedom.
- **41. What's Wrong with Colonialism** [ch55]. Acquisition, settlement, civilizing missions, and a cosmopolitan critique (Lea Ypi's chapter, per the 3rd-edition TOC tradition; author line not verified in extraction head).

## Part IX — International Affairs (chs. 42–49)
- **42. Just War** — G. E. M. Anscombe [ch57]. "The Justice of the Present War Examined" (1939) and "Mr Truman's Degree" (1957).
- **43. National Self-determination** — Avishai Margalit and Joseph Raz [ch58]. Isolating the right to self-determination and its bearers.
- **44. The Ethical Significance of Nationality** — David Miller [ch59]. Defends ethically significant national boundaries and special duties to compatriots.
- **45. The Romance of the Nation-State** — David Luban [ch60]. Short chapter (14k chars — the briefest body file in the folder); rights-based critique of nation-state war-making.
- **46. Democracy** — David Held [ch61]. Models of democracy from city-states to a cosmopolitan order under globalization.
- **47. Transnationalizing the Public Sphere** — Nancy Fraser [ch62]. Rethinking the public sphere beyond the Westphalian frame.
- **48. Migration and Poverty** — Thomas Pogge [ch63]. Against the view that the rich owe no weighty responsibility to foreigners in desperate need.
- **49. Humanity and Justice in Global Perspective** — Brian Barry [ch64]. Humanity requires aid; justice requires transfers; in detail the obligations converge.

## Coverage
Full text present for all 49 chapters and read at chapter-head level for this map; read in full for chs. 11 (Dahl), 15 (Rawls — the 1958 paper), and 16 (O'Neill on Nozick), which feed this slice's entries. Defects are labelling, not content: spine file titles are unreliable ("References" files carry body text), chapters split across files, and the folder's short name omits "An Anthology," which is what caused the Kymlicka misidentification corrected above. Ch. 45's file is unusually short and may be an excerpt rather than the full chapter.
