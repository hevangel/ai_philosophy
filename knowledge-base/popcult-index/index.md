# The Philosophy–Pop Culture Reverse Index

Most pop-philosophy books work forward: take an episode, tell what happened, then attach a philosopher. This index works backward. It is organized by **philosopher and concept** — for each one you get a short statement of the idea, then the book chapters that apply it to a pop-culture work, each with a note on what that chapter actually argues.

The whole index lives in the interactive two-pane explorer — open **Philosophy through Pop Culture** on the site (the `/popcult` route) and browse any philosopher, concept, book, or university course.

## Coverage

- Corpus: **271 books · 10123 chapters** (Open Court *Popular Culture and Philosophy*, Wiley-Blackwell *… and Philosophy*, and friends)
- Chapters read chapter-by-chapter so far: **10123 of 10123** (100%)
- Chapters mapped to index entries: **5810**
- Concepts in the index: **485** (all with intros) · Philosophers: **264** (all with intros)
- University courses: **18** from 7 universities, tiered by how much material is public — **7** with full free course videos (tier A), **8** with substantial partial material (tier B), **3** with readings or listings only (tier C)
- Lectures indexed: **286**, from the public syllabi of **16** of the 18 courses, each with a summary of what it teaches and the concepts (**119** of them) and philosophers (**47** of them) it uses

## How this was built

Every chapter was read in full by a dedicated reading pass. For each chapter the reader records which philosophers and concepts the chapter *actually uses* (not name-drops) and writes 2–4 sentences on what the chapter argues and how it connects the pop-culture material to the philosophy. Chapters that fit no existing index entry are logged as gaps; the index grows to absorb them. Coverage numbers above are exact counts of what has been read so far — never estimates.

The university courses were verified by hand against the original university pages (October 2026). Each record links only to officially public material — lecture videos, transcripts, notes, slides, reading lists, syllabi — and never redistributes it; the tier records honestly how much of the course a self-learner can actually access, and unofficial uploads are flagged as such. Where a university publishes its syllabus, every lecture is indexed from that official listing — title and order as published, a summary that never claims more than the official description supports, and philosopher/concept links only where the lecture substantively teaches them (titles-only listings get titles only; two courses publish no session listing at all and say so). Course pages link into the index, and every concept or philosopher page lists the exact lectures across the courses where it is taught.
