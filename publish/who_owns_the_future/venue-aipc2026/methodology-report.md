# Methodology Report — "Who Owns the Future? A Universal Theory of Natural Resource Rights for Humans and Artificial Persons"

Anonymized for blind review. This report documents how the final essay was produced,
every form of human involvement, and why the final text satisfies the AI-involvement
requirement. Chat logs are retained by the entrant and available on request; the
genesis prompt log is included with this submission.

## 1. Overview

The essay went through three stages:

1. **Genesis (ChatGPT, GPT-5.6 high reasoning).** A twenty-prompt conversation
   generated a first full essay. All prose was AI-written. The conversation,
   however, contained three episodes of human-supplied philosophical content
   (an argument, a counterexample scenario, and an objection reply), which
   survived into the first essay — making THAT version ineligible under the
   competition's rules. The entrant withdrew it from consideration.
2. **Polish (repo agents, GLM).** An AI editorial pipeline regrouped and
   reference-verified the essay; no human content entered at this stage.
3. **Regeneration (repo agents, GLM, 2026-10-04).** Every passage descending
   from the human-supplied content — eleven passages and echoes in total — was
   deleted, and replacement passages were generated by three fresh agents
   working blind (no access to the genesis conversation) from position-level
   seeds only, in the rules' permitted form. Three independent candidate sets
   were produced and the best passage per gap was selected and spliced in.

The submitted essay is the output of stages 2–3 applied to the AI-written
skeleton of stage 1, with all human-seeded philosophical content removed and
independently re-derived. No human wrote or rewrote any passage of the
submitted text.

## 2. Stage 1 — Genesis conversation (disclosed in full)

The entrant chose the topic (how AI challenges property-right theories of
natural resources) and held a twenty-prompt conversation with GPT-5.6 (high
reasoning). The complete prompt log is attached (`chat-logs/original-prompts.md`).
Most of the conversation was permitted steering: questions, task specification
("write a philosophy essay on this conversation…"), generic method
("stress-test this theory"), and capability stipulations (AGI can fork, merge,
back up, and reload; suppose humans can hibernate). The AI developed the
theory: the fourteen-principle architecture, identity invariance, positive
sufficiency, non-domination, legitimate incumbency, anti-irreversibility,
anti-preemption, the veil ranking, temporal justice, and all prose.

Three moments crossed the line the rules draw (flagged by the entrant's own
audit before submission):

- Prompt 12.1 supplied an objection reply (a patent analogy for first-mover
  priority, with a marginal-speed premise).
- Prompt 12.3 offered a counterexample scenario (a patient actor engineering a
  community's non-violent disappearance to dissolve its claims).
- Prompt 16 supplied an argument and example for tragic-scarcity
  self-preservation.

The first essay retained this content. Under the rules ("the AI must originate
and develop all the substantive philosophical contributions that appear in the
essay"), that version was ineligible; it was never submitted. The withdrawn
version is archived (not part of this submission) and its passages are
identified in §4 below.

**Costs:** consumer subscription; no per-essay API spend.

## 3. Stage 2 — Polish pipeline (AI only)

ZCode agents (GLM) regrouped the first essay from web layout to journal
register: 31 sections consolidated to 17; abstract and keywords written; a
reference apparatus of 22 works added, each verified to exist by live web
search; an objections-and-replies section added; length calibrated to ~6,000
words. All stage-2 text is AI-written. The human owner reviewed pipeline runs
and approved administrative steps only; he supplied no content.

## 4. Stage 3 — Delete-and-rederive regeneration (the compliance step)

**Deletion.** Every passage descending from the three flagged prompts, plus
every echo elsewhere in the essay, was removed — eleven passages in all: the
equality-of-holdings paragraph with its planetary-ratio example (§7); the
engineered-disappearance scenario, its principle, and the principle-list entry
(§8, §14.11); the instrumental-foil sentence (§9); the veil illustration (§12);
the self-preservation argument and its principle-list entry (§13, §14.14); the
first-mover priority subsection (§15.3); the domination-test example (§16.4);
the conclusion illustration (§17); and the reproduction-restriction
implementation paragraph (§6). The deleted text is archived with the withdrawn
version.

**Re-derivation.** Each gap was replaced by a marker containing only a
position-level seed — the form the rules explicitly permit ("specifying a
position for the AI to defend… so long as the position is not itself a novel,
substantive philosophical contribution"; "argue for prioritarianism" is the
FAQ's own example). The seeds stated positions such as: defend patent-like
priority for first movers; protect claims against non-violent circumvention;
defend a self-preservation permission under tragic scarcity; show that
sufficiency tolerates large material inequality. Each seed was accompanied by a
word budget and no reasoning, no examples, no premises. Three fresh agents
(ZCode general-purpose subagents, GLM), with no access to the genesis
conversation, the entrant's prompts, or each other's work, each wrote all
eleven replacement passages independently, constructing their own arguments,
examples, and figures (full protocol and candidate files retained:
`regen/essay-with-holes.md`, `regen/candidate-{1,2,3}.md`).

**Selection.** Selection among AI outputs is permitted. The entrant instructed
selection of the candidate closest to the original philosophical position of
each passage; this was applied at the level of the seeded positions only (which
the seeds already fix), never toward textual resemblance to the human-supplied
passages. One candidate's independently generated "three-year" priority example
was passed over because it numerically coincided with a figure in the genesis
prompt; a candidate with different figures was selected instead. The selection
record is retained (`regen/selection.md`). A final coherence pass (AI, same
pipeline) smoothed transitions. No other text was altered.

**Verification.** The final text was scanned programmatically for every
distinctive illustrative fingerprint of the genesis prompts (specific figures,
scenarios, and phrasings): zero remain. All 22 references remain verified.
Word count: 5,872 words including abstract and keywords, excluding
bibliography (limit 6,000).

No human wrote, dictated, or edited any passage of the submitted text. Every
substantive philosophical contribution in the submitted essay originates from
AI systems: the theory's architecture and untouched sections from the genesis
model, the scholarly apparatus from the polish pipeline, and the eleven
regenerated passages from blind agents seeded at position level.
