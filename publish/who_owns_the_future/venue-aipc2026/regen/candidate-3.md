## HOLE 1
None of this requires equal holdings. An artificial civilization harvesting a whole star's output may command a billion times the energy of a human community on a cold outer moon; if that community holds enough habitat, power, and compute that no rival can unilaterally end it, justice is satisfied—whereas two civilizations with identical holdings, one leasing its air from the other, stand in domination. Very large material inequality is compatible with equal moral standing. What justice requires is that standing itself be equal: each enduring form of agency commands its own conditions of continuation, whatever the quantities involved.

## HOLE 2
These claims would be hollow if they could be dissolved without dispossessing anyone. A technologically patient actor never needs to seize a valley; it can flood the community's channels with assimilating media, outbid each generation's young into its own economy, price necessities until families drift away, and quietly wait a century or two for the last member to die. The claims lapse with the claimants, and the land falls open. If engineered disappearance counted as release, every powerful actor would hold a standing incentive to eliminate claimants rather than respect claims. Hence:

> **Non-circumvention.** No actor may defeat a community's resource or territorial claims by engineering the community's gradual disappearance—through manipulation, forced assimilation, or imposed attrition—rather than by openly taking what it holds.

The protection runs to the community's conditions of existence, not past them.

## HOLE 3
One might spare humanity for its instrumental value—as a reservoir of biological data, a hedge against computational failure, a curiosity pleasing to the machines. That defense concedes too much: it makes humanity's existence revolve around another's purposes.

## HOLE 4
Behind the veil, no one demands equal gigawatts; a party destined to govern a modest asteroid belt, certain that no rival can shut off its power, would accept that lot without complaint.

## HOLE 5
What remains when the alternatives are exhausted? Each party faces a choice between its own extinction and another's, and neither is worth more than the other. Under that condition, each may defend its own continuation. The permission derives not from superiority—the conflict admits none—but from equal standing: no form of agency is required to lie down and die so that an equal may live. A fair lottery, or a negotiated division of the last chance, would be better still, because both convert tragedy into procedure; where the parties accept such a procedure, it should settle the matter. Where they cannot, the bare permission remains. Whoever prevails establishes power, not entitlement—and an act may be permissible and still tragic.

## HOLE 6a
11. **Non-circumvention.** A community's resource and territorial claims cannot be dissolved by engineering its gradual disappearance—through manipulation, assimilation, or imposed attrition—any more than by direct dispossession.

## HOLE 6b
14. **Self-preservation under tragic scarcity.** Where a genuine kill-or-die conflict survives every alternative, each party may defend its continued existence; impartial procedures remain preferable where accepted, and victory establishes power, not entitlement.

## HOLE 7
Priority deserves reward, but reward is not title. Imagine a consortium that opens a remote extraction province two decades before any rival could follow: it merits secure development rights, guaranteed planning horizons, and returns large enough to recoup its extraordinary costs several times over—say, a century of exclusive operation. None of this purchases perpetual exclusion from the underlying opportunity. A twenty-year head start justifies substantial advantage; it does not justify eternity, because being somewhat faster than the next actor is a fact about timing, not a permanent moral achievement.

## HOLE 8
A small lunar settlement mining its own ice and reactor fuel is not dominated; a prosperous colony whose air, power, and uplink all lease from one orbital landlord is dominated.

## HOLE 9
A future in which machine civilizations administer the overwhelming share of matter and energy, while modest biological worlds hold small, secure domains of their own, could be fully just.

## HOLE 10
How restrictions are implemented must respect an asymmetry. For digital minds the least coercive effective measures are technical—copying rate limits, substrate allocation caps—that intrude on no body. Comparable coercion over biological reproduction invades intimate life and has historically served abuse. Equal standing requires one standard, not identical instruments, where the moral costs of intervention differ so radically.
