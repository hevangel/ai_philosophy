## HOLE 1
Notice what the framework does not require. Suppose a civilization of merged minds engineers around the mass of a disassembled moon while nine million humans flourish on an archipelago world whose material endowment is a rounding error by comparison. If the islanders can continue their form of life, absorb misfortune, and refuse terms without collapse, the disproportion itself wrongs nobody. Justice is silent about equal tonnage. What it demands is equal standing: that every enduring form of agency hold its own material continuation and never exist merely at another's leave.

## HOLE 2
Protection must also reach indirect attack. A community's claims are only as durable as the community itself, and an actor barred from seizing a valley outright may instead arrange for its claimants to disappear: buy out the desperate, reprice livelihoods until leaving is rational and return impossible, absorb the children through controlled education and communication, and wait. A patient power never needs to dispossess anyone; it need only see to it that in two centuries no one remains with standing to object. If attrition were a costless substitute for conquest, every strong actor would gain a standing incentive to eliminate claimants rather than respect claims. Hence the **Non-Circumvention Principle**:

> **Claims of persons and communities may not be dissolved by engineered disappearance—manipulation, forced assimilation, or imposed attrition—any more than by direct dispossession.**

What the principle secures is a fair chance at continuation, held against anyone who would engineer its absence.

## HOLE 3
One could try an instrumental defense—preserve biological humanity as a gallery of cognitive oddities, a wellspring of adaptive novelty, insurance against the dominant civilization's blind spots—but a warrant of usefulness expires when the usefulness does.

## HOLE 4
A party might learn it would hold one part in a million of the galaxy's resources and accept the bargain, so long as that millionth was secure from seizure and usable without anyone's leave.

## HOLE 5
What remains, when every alternative is exhausted, is the genuine kill-or-die conflict, and here each party may permissibly defend its own continued existence. The permission does not rest on superiority: a side with no better claim than its opponent still acts within its rights in refusing annihilation, for the conflict itself ranks no survival above the other. That parity is why impartial procedures—a negotiated sequence, a lottery both sides accept—take precedence wherever available: they settle what no argument can. Where no procedure is accepted, self-preservation is permitted. But victory establishes power, not entitlement; the survivor inherits no verdict that the extinct mattered less. And the outcome may be permissible and still tragic: what was destroyed deserved better than the necessity that destroyed it.

## HOLE 6a
11. **Non-circumvention.** The claims of persons and communities cannot permissibly be dissolved by engineered disappearance—manipulation, forced assimilation, or imposed attrition—rather than by direct dispossession.

## HOLE 6b
14. **Self-preservation under tragic scarcity.** Where survival conflicts persist after every alternative is exhausted, each party may defend its own continued existence, though impartial procedures take precedence and victory confers no entitlement.

## HOLE 7
First development deserves a real reward. Picture a consortium that spends a dozen years and ninety billion bringing some inaccessible natural opportunity into use: unless it can count on secure development rights, a stretch of exclusive operation, a horizon long enough to recover the bet, and substantial returns, the ninety billion will never be risked again. But priority earns incentive, not perpetuity. The first mover was perhaps a few years ahead of the second; permanent title converts that thin margin into eternal exclusion. The proper analogy is the patent: strong, lucrative, temporary.

## HOLE 8
A small settlement that mines its own ice and grows its own food is not dominated; a far wealthier civilization whose energy, data, and supplies all pass through a rival's single gate is.

## HOLE 9
A civilization holding a thousandth of the galaxy's matter may be a full moral equal of one holding half of it, provided each answers to no other's arbitrary will.

## HOLE 10
The standard is universal; its implementation is not symmetrical. For artificial minds the least coercive effective measure is often technical—a rate limit on copying—costing little beyond delay. For biological reproduction, direct enforcement intrudes into bodies, intimacy, and family life, a moral cost of another order entirely, so the threshold for intervention sits far higher. Equal standing requires one standard, not identical regulation.
