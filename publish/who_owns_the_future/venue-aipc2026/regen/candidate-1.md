## HOLE 1
Positive sufficiency is deliberately tolerant of inequality. Imagine a system-spanning artificial civilization harvesting the output of a thousand stars beside a human population running on the energy of one; if that single share is secured—entrenched, redundant, beyond unilateral revocation—the humans are not wronged by the ratio. What sufficiency forbids is not disparity but precarity. A doctrine demanding equal quantities would collapse back into person-counting at the scale of civilizations. The equality justice requires is standing: each enduring form of agency enters the resource order as a principal, never as a tenant whose lease another may terminate.

## HOLE 2
Claims so grounded face a second threat: strategies that never dispossess anyone. A community's claims endure only as long as the community does, and a dominant actor can end a community without confiscating a single hectare—buying out the young, restructuring incentives until emigration is each individual's rational choice, shaping the information environment until the culture loses its carriers, or simply waiting a few generations while attrition finishes the work. Every step can look consensual; the destination is a vanished claimant. If claims bound only against direct seizure, a patient power would gain a standing incentive to eliminate claimants rather than respect claims, so the protection must extend to the mechanism of removal:

> **Non-Circumvention.** A community's resource and territorial claims may not be defeated by engineered disappearance—manipulation, induced assimilation, or imposed attrition—any more than by armed expulsion.

## HOLE 3
One reply is instrumental: preserve humanity as a warehouse of biological intuition, a control group for machine hypotheses, an insurance policy against digital failure. But a right to exist earned by usefulness is no right; it lapses when the use does.

## HOLE 4
A party might accept the lot of a civilization commanding a billionth of the accessible matter, provided its independence were secured against every larger power.

## HOLE 5
What remains when the alternatives are exhausted? Neither party to a genuine kill-or-die conflict is worth more than the other; the ordinary asymmetry of self-defense, in which the innocent resist the unjust, is absent, and each faces an equal claim. Each may therefore act to preserve itself. The permission is not a verdict that the victor deserved to win: necessity establishes control, not entitlement, and the survivor inherits no title beyond what its later incumbency and dependence can themselves support. Because the permission is symmetric and its exercise terrible, impartial procedures—a negotiated division, a lottery both sides accept—remain strictly preferable wherever available. The outcome can be permissible and still tragic; the permission cancels no part of the loss.

## HOLE 6b
14. **Self-preservation under tragic scarcity.** Where a genuine kill-or-die conflict survives every alternative, each party may act to preserve its own existence; victory confers control, not moral superiority or permanent title.

## HOLE 7
Genuine priority deserves reward. The first developer of a difficult site—years of surveying, failed ventures, then a working extraction system—creates the opportunity imitators would merely copy. Secure development rights, substantial returns, a long stretch of exclusive operation, planning horizons over decades: all are justified, since without them the pioneering effort never happens. What it does not deserve is permanent title. Being three years ahead of the next consortium merits reward proportioned to the lead—and the lead was speed, not creation of the deposit itself.

## HOLE 8
A colony running on its own small reactor is materially modest yet not dominated; a prosperous polity whose energy, coolant, and spare parts all pass through a rival's chokepoints is comfortable yet dominated.

## HOLE 9
A galaxy in which artificial civilizations command trillions of times the energy available to humanity can be fully just, provided humanity's survival is no one's to revoke.

## HOLE 10
Implementation tracks the same standard by different routes. Rate-limiting the instantiation of new digital minds is a technical measure—coercive, but not an invasion of anyone's body—whereas the least coercive effective means for biological reproduction are education, contraception, and material support. Equal standing requires the same standard, not the same instruments, where the moral costs of intervention differ this much.
