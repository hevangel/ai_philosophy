# Deposit note — PhilArchive

**Title:** Who Owns the Future? A Universal Theory of Natural Resource Rights for Humans
and Artificial Persons
**Author:** Horace Chan (independent scholar)
**Word count:** ~6,100 (5,700 body)
**Format:** PDF (as required)

**Two-sentence summary:** The essay argues that familiar theories of property tacitly
assume one biological architecture of personhood, and that forking, merging, hibernating,
and digitally reproducing persons would break per-capita, first-possession, labor, and
productivity-based allocation rules alike. It develops Universal Resource Stewardship —
fourteen principles combining identity invariance, sufficiency for non-dominated
continuation, legitimate incumbency, anti-preemption, and non-circumvention — and applies
the theory to present-day scarcity rents, depletion, territory, inheritance, and
chokepoint control.

of the essay exists (hevangel.github.io), which PhilArchive's policy does not treat as
