**Title:** The Summit Is Not the Climb: AI, Creative Value, and the Displacement Fallacy

**Subtitle:** A Reply to Jordan MacKenzie on AI and Valuing Practices

**Author:** Horace Chan

**Type:** Article (reply/commentary)

**Relation:** This paper is a reply to Jordan MacKenzie, "Fear and Loathing in Silicon Valley:
Does AI Threaten Our Valuing Practices?", *Philosophers' Imprint* 26 (25): 1–13 (2026),
https://doi.org/10.3998/phimp.7153 (PhilArchive record: philarchive.org/rec/MACFAL-5).
Please link the deposit to that record as a reply.

**Abstract:** Jordan MacKenzie argues that generative artificial intelligence poses two
existential threats to human valuing practices: displacement of meaningful creative processes
by merely functional products, and revelation that audiences do not share creators'
commitments. This paper challenges the inferences underlying both claims. It distinguishes
instrumental from constitutive difficulty, defends a principle of evaluative relevance across
productive, receptive, relational, and institutional value, distinguishes recognition from
ratification, and — drawing on Foucault and Bourdieu — separates the loss of cultural
exclusivity from the loss of intrinsic value. The resulting argument places a justificatory
burden on claims that automation constitutes value destruction.
