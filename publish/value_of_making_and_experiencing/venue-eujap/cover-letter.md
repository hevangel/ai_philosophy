# Cover letter — EUJAP submission (Article)

Dear Editors,

I am submitting "The Summit Is Not the Climb: AI, Creative Value, and the Displacement
Fallacy — A Reply to Jordan MacKenzie on AI and Valuing Practices" for consideration as an
Article (approx. 7,200 words including references).

The paper is a critical discussion of Jordan MacKenzie, "Fear and Loathing in Silicon Valley:
Does AI Threaten Our Valuing Practices?", *Philosophers' Imprint* 26 (25): 1–13 (2026).
MacKenzie argues that generative AI poses two existential threats to human valuing practices:
a forward-looking threat of displacing meaningful creative processes with merely functional
products, and a backward-looking threat in which audiences' indifference to creative
provenance reveals a troubling discord in values. The paper challenges the inferences
underlying both claims. It distinguishes instrumental from constitutive difficulty (the
"displacement fallacy"), defends a principle of evaluative relevance across productive,
receptive, relational, and institutional value, distinguishes recognition from ratification
in the ethics of aesthetic response, and — drawing on Foucault's author-function and
Bourdieu's account of distinction — separates the loss of cultural exclusivity from the loss
of intrinsic value. The conclusion states a burden-of-proof requirement for displacement
claims: technological replacement should be assessed by its effects on the values displaced
practices served.

The paper is submitted under the Article category (within the 9,000-word limit) rather than
the Discussion format, as its length exceeds 5,000 words; its genre remains a critical
discussion of recently published work.
