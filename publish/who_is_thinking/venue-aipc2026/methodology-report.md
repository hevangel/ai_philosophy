# Methodology Report

**Essay submitted:** "Who Is Thinking? Artificial Intelligence, Authorship, and the
Purpose of Philosophy"

*This report was drafted with GLM-5.3 (flash) from the entrant's prompt log and records;
the entrant reviewed and approved it. All names have been redacted for anonymized review.*

## 1. Overview

The essay was produced in three stages, all text ultimately written by AI systems:

1. **Origin (human context, no essay text):** a public Facebook exchange between the
   entrant and a philosophy-professor friend about AI and philosophical writing. This
   exchange supplied the topic and the motivating tension; it is not part of the essay.
2. **Initial essay:** GPT-5.6 ("Sol", high thinking effort, ChatGPT app) wrote a
   college-level philosophy essay from the entrant's conversation with it — three prompts
   in total (verbatim in §3), with the Facebook exchange pasted as context.
3. **Development into a publishable paper:** AI agents in the entrant's AI-maintained
   repository (GLM-5.3, running the repository's documented `phil-polish` skill — see §4)
   verified all references, added the scholarly apparatus, and rewrote the draft to
   academic register.

No sentence of the submitted manuscript was written by a human. The entrant's role was
topic selection, generic prompting, and running the AI agent pipeline.

## 2. Origin: the Facebook exchange (verbatim, redacted)

The essay exists because of this exchange. The professor friend posted (Chinese, translated
below):

> 【再談 AI 與哲學寫作】那次跟友人聊到 AI 與哲學寫作，我還說：「肯定已有不少哲學人利用 AI 協助寫作，尤其是一些英文非母語者。」我不反對利用 AI 協助寫作，更不反對與 AI 討論哲學（我自己就經常這樣做），但假如寫作內容主要來自 AI，英文主要是 AI 寫的，而且很多人這樣做，然後投稿到期刊，加上很多人用 AI 評審論文，我認為終歸會摧毀整個哲學期刊論文的出版機制。我相信在不久的將來，這便會發生。
>
> 【意興闌珊】早兩天跟友人聊起 AI 與哲學寫作，分享經驗，然後我說：「我那麼喜歡寫作，也有點感到意興闌珊。」當時沒有解釋此話的意思，現在說說。雖然現時 AI 還不能獨立寫出最高水平的哲學論文或專書，但相信數年內可以做到，甚至往後會有革命性的創見，超越最傑出的哲學家。即使以現時的 AI 來說，我的經驗是：跟 AI 討論，它不但能輕易指出我文字上的毛病，還能乾脆利落擊中我論證裏的弱點；我據之修正，自然能改進論文，可是，這同時也令我感到自己的哲學能力很有限。我說的意興闌珊，就是自覺「我不外如是，不寫也罷」，這是我從未有過的感覺，因為跟同儕相比，我一向是蠻有自信的。往下該如何走呢？我真的不知道。然而，這沒有引起焦慮，因為我的人生還有很多方面足以令我感到活得充實和精彩。

> *(Translation: "More on AI and philosophical writing: … I don't object to AI-assisted
> writing, nor to discussing philosophy with AI — I do it often myself. But if the content
> mainly comes from AI and the English is mainly AI-written, and many people do this and
> submit to journals, and many people use AI to review papers, I believe it will eventually
> destroy the whole journal-publication mechanism. I believe this will happen in the near
> future." / "Dispirited: … Although AI cannot yet independently write the highest-level
> philosophy papers or books, I believe it will within a few years, and may later produce
> revolutionary insights surpassing the most outstanding philosophers. Even with current
> AI, my experience is that in discussion it not only easily points out flaws in my prose
> but squarely hits weaknesses in my arguments; correcting accordingly improves my papers —
> yet it also makes me feel my philosophical ability is quite limited. 'Dispirited' means
> realizing 'I am nothing special; I might as well not write.' I had never had this
> feeling, as among my peers I have always been rather confident. Where to go from here? I
> honestly don't know. Yet it has caused no anxiety, because many other aspects of my life
> make it feel full and exciting.")*

The entrant replied:

> 我這些哲學業餘, 有了AI後, 終於也可以寫出跟專業水平的哲學文章
>
> *(Translation: "Philosophy amateurs like me — with AI, we can finally write philosophy
> articles at a professional level.")*

The professor replied:

> 根本不是你寫，不要自欺欺人。
>
> *(Translation: "It is not you writing at all. Don't deceive yourself.")*

The entrant replied:

> 成品內容比誰寫的重要。只要我真的看得明生成出來文章, 別人問我懂答, 可以繼續在這方向與AI協同寫新文章, 東西是否全部從我腦中想出來, 有關係嗎？
>
> *(Translation: "The content of the finished product matters more than who wrote it. As
> long as I genuinely understand the generated article, can answer when others ask me
> about it, and can keep co-writing new articles with AI in this direction — does it
> matter whether everything came out of my own brain?")*

**Disclosure note (grey area).** The entrant's two short replies articulate a stance — the
finished product's content matters more than the provenance of each idea, provided the
human understands and can answer for it. This exchange was pasted into the ChatGPT
conversation as context before the prompts below. The entrant did not instruct the AI to
defend this stance, did not critique any AI output, and the essay's central thesis
(*epistemic agency*, which requires more than the entrant's "product + understanding"
condition) was developed by the AI systems. We disclose the exchange verbatim so the
organizers can weigh whether it crosses from "choosing the topic of the essay" (which we
take it to be) into supplying philosophical ideas.

## 4. Development into the submitted paper — repository agents (GLM-5.3)

The entrant runs a static philosophy website maintained end-to-end by AI agents. The GPT-5.6
essay was processed by the repository's agent pipeline (GLM-5.3) in two AI-to-AI stages:

### 4.1 Site publication pass (2026-09-10)

The repository is AI-maintained end-to-end: human collaborators read, AI agents do all
editing, following written repository conventions (AGENTS.md). Following those
conventions, the repo's agents adapted the GPT-5.6 essay into a web article — dialogue-
derived drafting, structure, cover text — and published it on the philosophy site
(2,930 words, 2026-09-10). No specialized skill existed in the repository at this date;
the step followed the repo's standing content conventions.

### 4.2 Academic polish pass (2026-09-11) — the `phil-polish` skill

The polish stage was not ad hoc prompting: it was executed by an AI agent (GLM-5.3) running
a reusable, documented skill in the repository — `phil-polish` — one of a suite of
submission-pipeline skills defined in the repository's agent instructions (AGENTS.md).
`phil-polish` specifies a fixed procedure for upgrading a draft to philosophy-journal
standard. The skill is argument-agnostic infrastructure: its procedure is the same for any
draft, and its central rule is that the argument must remain the draft's own — the skill
strengthens and clarifies the existing thesis, and never replaces it with a different or
"safer" one. As applied in this run, its steps were:

1. **Clean.** Strip chat-export artifacts and debris; preserve the draft's structure.
2. **Structural pass.** Assess whether one clear thesis is stated early and defended in
   identifiable steps; check that each section contributes; ensure the paper names its
   strongest opponents and answers them — the draft lacked an objections-and-replies
   section, so one (§10, three objections with replies) was constructed by the agent from
   material already present in the draft.
3. **Scholarly apparatus.** Write an abstract (150–250 words), keywords, and a road-map
   paragraph; convert citations to author–date; verify every citation against the live
   literature via web search (title, author, year, journal; DOI where findable). All 11
   references were verified; 2 were added by the agent after verification (Clark & Chalmers
   1998; Gerlich 2025). The skill explicitly forbids inventing or silently retaining
   unverifiable references.
4. **Language pass.** Academic register, removal of LLM filler, consistent spelling
   dialect, varied rhythm.
5. **Length calibration** toward journal norms.
6. **AI-use log.** The skill requires a factual log of everything the AI changed, written
   to `polish-notes.md` — the honest record of this run's changes, quoted in the polish
   log's own wording: "All philosophical theses, examples … and the overall argument
   structure originate from the human author's draft", where "the draft" is the **GPT-5.6
   essay** from §3, not human writing (the log's phrase "human author" refers to
   authorship-of-record on the website; the draft's text was AI-generated per §3).

The entrant's involvement in this stage was operating the agent tools and selecting the
output — no philosophical contribution. The skill-based pipeline (agentic scaffolds,
documented procedures, citation-verification tooling) is argument-agnostic scaffolding in
the competition's permitted sense: it would run identically on any essay on any topic.

methodology: every substantive stage of this essay's production is accounted for in §3
and §4 above.
