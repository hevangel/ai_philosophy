### Preface

That thinking matters in a human life goes without saying. But thinking needs method if thought is to be clear, flexible, and acute. As Confucius said: "If a craftsman wants to do his work well, he must first sharpen his tools."

Rational disputation is excellent training for thinking. Provided it does not resort to sophistry, the saying "truth becomes clearer the more it is debated" can hold.

In both "thinking" and "disputation," Li Tianming's attainments are well known among Hong Kong university students and indeed the whole elite stratum. This book collects representative works of his in these two areas as its "Main Part."

Besides thinking about methods of thinking, Mr Li also thinks about the problems of human life. Some of his views on thinking methods and on life's problems, and even his distinctive style of handling affairs, can be glimpsed in the several interviews with him collected in this book's "Introductory Part."

Finally, Mr Li's critical disputation on religion has stirred a great many responses and no small shock; a selection of these is gathered into the book's "Reference Part."

— The Editors
