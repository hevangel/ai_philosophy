- **1. The Garden of Eden** — the two opening questions (who are you? where does the world come from?) and wonder as philosophy's starting point.
- **2. The Top Hat** — the world as a white rabbit pulled from a magician's hat; philosophy as staying astonished; Alberto's course begins.
- **3. The Myths** — Norse and Greek mythology as pre-philosophical explanation; the shift from *mythos* to *logos*.
- **4. The Natural Philosophers** — the pre-Socratics (Thales, Anaximander, Heraclitus, Parmenides) seeking a basic substance behind change.
- **5. Democritus** — atomism: indivisible atoms in the void; the materialist answer to change vs. the eternal.
- **6. Fate** — Greek fatalism, the Delphic oracle, "know thyself"; Herodotus and the limits of destiny.
- **7. Socrates** — the Socratic method (irony, midwifery), trial and death; "the wisest is she who knows she does not know."
- **8. Athens** — classical Athens: democracy and the Sophists (Protagoras) as context for Socrates and Plato (largely plot).
- **9. Plato** — the theory of Forms, the myth of the cave, the immortal soul, the Academy.
- **10. The Major's Cabin** — largely plot; the frame mystery deepens.
- **11. Aristotle** — logic, the four causes, teleology, classification; his critique of Plato's Forms.
- **12. Hellenism** — Cynicism, Stoicism, Epicureanism, Neoplatonism, mysticism: the post-Alexander meeting of Greek and Eastern thought.
- **13. The Postcards** — largely plot; thematically stages the Berkeley-style question of whether Sophie's world is authored.
- **14. Two Cultures** — the Judeo-Christian and Greco-Roman roots of the West: monotheism, Jesus, Paul carrying Christianity to the Greek world.
- **15. The Middle Ages** — Augustine's fusing of Platonism and Christianity; Aquinas' "Christianization" of Aristotle.
- **16. The Renaissance** — humanism, the new science (Copernicus, Kepler, Galileo, Newton), the Reformation, measurable nature.
- **17. The Baroque** — 17th-century tensions: life as theatre/dream, *vanitas*, puritanism, absolutism — setting up Descartes.
- **18. Descartes** — rationalism: methodical doubt, *cogito ergo sum*, mind–body dualism. → orientation for *External-World Skepticism* (epistemology).
- **19. Spinoza** — monism: *Deus sive Natura*, determinism, ethics through seeing necessity.
- **20. Locke** — British empiricism: the blank slate, sensation and reflection, primary vs. secondary qualities.
- **21. Hume** — impressions vs. ideas; scepticism about causation and the self; custom as habit, not reason. → orientation for *The Problem of Induction* (epistemology).
- **22. Berkeley** — *esse est percipi*; idealism and the world existing only in the mind of God — the philosophical hinge of the novel's plot.
- **23. Bjerkely** — almost entirely plot; dramatizes Berkeley's idealism and the Hilde/Sophie double-world structure.
- **24. The Enlightenment** — French Enlightenment: reason, human rights, opposition to authority and superstition (Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau), and revolution.
- **25. Kant** — the synthesis of rationalism and empiricism: the Copernican revolution of the mind, categories, the thing-in-itself, the categorical imperative.
- **26. Romanticism** — the world-soul, Romantic genius, art and nature as expressions of the Absolute (Schelling, Novalis).
- **27. Hegel** — dialectics; history as the unfolding of the world spirit; truth as historically situated.
- **28. Kierkegaard** — the individual against the system: subjectivity is truth, the stages of life, the leap of faith. → background for the existentialism entries (book 018).
- **29. Marx** — historical materialism: mode of production, class struggle, alienation, ideology as superstructure.
- **30. Darwin** — evolution by natural selection; the materialist account of life that displaces design arguments.
- **31. Freud** — the unconscious, id/ego/superego, dream interpretation.
- **32. Our Own Time** — 20th-century philosophy: existentialism (Sartre: existence precedes essence); UN/ecology themes. (Some editions title this "We Are the World's Stage" — edition-dependent, per the reconstruction.) → orientation for *Existence Precedes Essence* (this subfield).
- **33. The Garden Party** — largely plot: the Midsummer Eve party and the escape trick.
- **34. Counterpoint** — the narrative shifts to Hilde's world; the frame's "counterpoint" mirrors the question of which world is real.
- **35. The Big Bang** — cosmology and the origin of the universe; the book closes where philosophy began: how the world came out of nothing.
