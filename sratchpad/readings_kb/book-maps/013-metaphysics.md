# Book map — 013: Helen Beebee & Nigel Sabbarton-Leary (eds.), *The Semantics and Metaphysics of Natural Kinds* (Routledge, 2010)

*Book layer · Metaphysics slice. ★ = chapter feeds one of this slice's problem entries. Status tags describe the corpus file, not the reading itself.*

**Extraction note — read this first: this is not the book the catalogue says it is.** The folder (`data/philosophy_readings/markdown/013 - Metaphysics/`) was commissioned and described — in PLAN.md and in this slice's brief — as Richard Taylor, *Metaphysics*, and every one of its 21 files carries the H1 / filename title "The Semantics and Metaphysics of Natural Kinds". On inspection the files are **not mis-titled chapters of Taylor**: they are a complete, correctly titled extraction of a different book altogether — the Beebee & Sabbarton-Leary edited volume *The Semantics and Metaphysics of Natural Kinds* (Routledge Studies in Metaphysics, first published 2010; copyright and title pages in files ch03–ch05 say so). There is **no Taylor text anywhere in the folder**: no chapter on fate, free will, causation in Taylor's sense, time, personal identity, or the self exists here, and none of those problem entries could be built from this book. The real "chapter titles" were therefore recovered not from Taylor but from this volume's own apparatus: the **Contents page (file ch06)** lists all twelve papers with authors, and each paper file carries its own real heading inside the text (e.g. file ch09 opens "2 Rigidity, Natural Kind Terms, and Metasemantics"). The map below uses the book's own paper numbering, with extraction file numbers in brackets.

**Quantification of the discrepancy.** 21 files; **all 21 (100%) share the identical filename/H1**, which is the *book's* title, not any chapter's. Of the 21: 7 are front matter (ch01–ch07), 12 are the papers themselves (ch08–ch19, each ~5,800–11,600 words, full text), and 2 are back matter (ch20 contributors, ch21 index). No paper text is missing, stubbed, or corrupt — this is the cleanest extraction in the pilot's experience; the defect is purely one of **cataloguing/attribution** (wrong author, wrong book, generic folder label "Metaphysics"), not of extraction. PLAN.md's data-quality note ("Taylor, *Metaphysics* (013) — every chapter file is titled…") should be corrected accordingly: the remedy is to re-catalogue 013 as Beebee & Sabbarton-Leary (2010), not to recover Taylor titles that were never here.

## The twelve papers

- **1. Introduction — Helen Beebee & Nigel Sabbarton-Leary** [file ch08; full text, ~11,600 words] ★ → *all six entries (survey chapter for the whole slice)*. §1, Classifying nature: the metaphysics perspective — what makes a kind *natural* (Quine on similarity and theoretical standards; "nargs"; Ellis's six a priori criteria; Boyd's homeostatic property clusters). §2, Natural kind semantics — Kripke on proper names against Frege–Russell (the modal, epistemological, and semantical arguments; baptism and direct reference), then the extension to natural kind terms. §3, Kripke, a posteriori necessity, and the metaphysics of natural kinds — theoretical identities, essence after Locke and Hume. §4, Putnam, incommensurability, and natural kinds. Also the volume's own summaries of every later paper, used here to cross-check each paper's thesis.
- **2. Rigidity, Natural Kind Terms, and Metasemantics — Corine Besson** [file ch09; full text, ~10,000 words] ★ → *Rigidity and Natural Kind Terms*. Rigidity trivializes for general terms on Kripke's definition; Besson's remedy is *de jure* obstinate rigidity grounded in the metasemantics of "dubbing": natural kind terms are introduced by name-acquiring transactions that other general terms cannot undergo.
- **3. General Terms as Designators: A Defence of The View — Genoveva Martí & José Martínez-Fernández** [file ch10; full text, ~9,100 words] ★ → *Rigidity and Natural Kind Terms*. General terms designate universals/kinds ("The View"); answers the trivialization problem, the overgeneralization problem (biting the bullet: all simple general terms behave alike semantically), and the necessity of theoretical identifications (kind vs exemplifier semantics).
- **4. Are Natural Kind Terms Special? — Åsa Wikforss** [file ch11; full text, ~9,700 words] ★ → *Rigidity and Natural Kind Terms*. No: neither rigidity nor non-descriptionality marks natural kind terms off semantically, and externalism does not rescue a special metasemantics either; a version of descriptivism is preferable, and any specialness these terms have comes from their role in scientific explanation.
- **5. The Commonalities between Proper Names and Natural Kind Terms: A Fregean Perspective — Harold Noonan** [file ch12; full text, ~10,400 words] ★ → *Rigidity and Natural Kind Terms*; supports *The Necessary A Posteriori*. Explains the Kripkean commonalities of names and kind terms within a Frege–Russell framework (the modal argument, propositional attitudes and deferential intentions, Twin Earth); rejects the necessary a posteriori for kind terms as a conflation of essence with contingent a posteriori thought.
- **6. Theoretical Identity Statements, Their Truth, and Their Discovery — Joseph LaPorte** [file ch13; full text, ~10,700 words] ★ → *Essences and Theoretical Identities*. Mixed verdict on the Kripke–Putnam tradition: theoretical identities can be *true* (clades such as Mammalia and Chordata have specifiable essences), but they are not *discovered* to be true — scientists stipulatively decide how to use traditional terms when inquiry is disrupted (whales/fish, birds/dinosaurs; conflicting delimitations of taxa; stipulation needed even given one agreed characterization).
- **7. Discovering the Essences of Natural Kinds — Alexander Bird** [file ch14; full text, ~6,100 words] ★ → *Essences and Theoretical Identities*. Reply to LaPorte: vernacular kind concepts leave far less room for precisifying decision than open texture suggests (the division of linguistic labour; D₂O and "water = H₂O"); many essential truths are not identities at all; and identities between scientific (non-vernacular) terms — e.g. nineteenth-century elements named before atomic structure was known — are discovered, not stipulated.
- **8. The Elements and Conceptual Change — Robin Findlay Hendry** [file ch15; full text, ~10,600 words] ★ → *Essences and Theoretical Identities*. Chemical kinds as the test case: what chemical kinds have to do, Lavoisier on the elements, elements before and after Lavoisier, whether element identity across theory change is discovery, and (§5) necessity and essence — sceptical that essence can be *deduced* from necessity (Fine's singleton case), but argues nuclear charge earns its individuating role through its explanatory, counternomic use in chemistry.
- **9. On the Abuse of the Necessary *A Posteriori* — Helen Beebee & Nigel Sabbarton-Leary** [file ch16; full text, ~9,800 words] ★ → *The Necessary A Posteriori*. Even granting Kripke on names and kind terms, extending the category needs argument case by case: a claim needs both a *Necessity* component (a Twin Earth–style judgement that the underlying nature is the essence) and an *A Posteriority* component (the term not introduced by stipulative definition). Brian Ellis's scientific essentialism — laws of nature as metaphysically necessary but a posteriori — is the worked example of the abuse: his extended kinds (substance kinds, process kinds) fail the model, undermining his de re necessity.
- **10. Crosscutting Natural Kinds and the Hierarchy Thesis — Emma Tobin** [file ch17; full text, ~5,900 words] ★ → *Classification and the Hierarchy Thesis*; supports *Natural Kinds*. The hierarchy thesis (overlapping kinds must nest as genus/species; Kuhn's no-overlap principle) fails in biology (mammals/quadrupeds) and even in paradigmatic chemistry (proteins/enzymes — albumin, renin, the hairpin ribozyme). With Khalidi and Dupré: reject the hierarchy thesis, but — *pace* Hacking — rejecting it does not entail conventionalism about kinds.
- **11. From Constitutional Necessities to Causal Necessities — Jessica Wilson** [file ch18; full text, ~9,100 words] ★ → *Causal Necessity and Hume's Dictum*. Humeans accept necessary connections between entities that are not wholly distinct (sets/members, fusions/parts, determinates/determinables, kind members/intrinsic properties) while denying, per Hume's Dictum, necessary *causal* connections between wholly distinct entities. Wilson's conditional — *Constitutional → Causal*: if one accepts certain constitutional necessities, one should accept certain causal necessities — argues the differential treatment is unprincipled: Schaffer's "compelling proof" criterion warrants necessities of identity but not the other constitutional necessities Humeans already accept, whose warrant is the same non-compelling, systematizing kind available in the causal case. The non-Humean framework faces no such tension.
- **12. Realism, Natural Kinds, and Philosophical Methods — Richard N. Boyd** [file ch19; full text, ~9,900 words] ★ → *Natural Kinds*. Scientific realism developed as thoroughgoing naturalism: the accommodation theory of reference, homeostatic property cluster (HPC) kinds (no precise membership conditions; species as paradigm), the discipline-relativity of naturalness, anti-foundationalist epistemology, natural kinds as in part social constructions without loss of mind-independence, and the consequences for philosophical method itself (against a priori conceptual analysis as a foundation).
