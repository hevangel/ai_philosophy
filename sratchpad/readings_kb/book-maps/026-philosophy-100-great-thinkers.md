# Book map — 026: Jeremy Harwood, *Philosophy: A Beginner's Guide to the Ideas of 100 Great Thinkers* (2010)

*Book layer · popular history-of-philosophy survey. Feeds the book layer and Connections; orientation material, not a scholarly source.*

**Extraction note.** The extracted folder (`data/philosophy_readings/markdown/026 - A Beginner's Guide to the Ideas of 100 Great Thinkers - Philosophy/`) holds 13 files, all titled simply "Philosophy": front matter and copyright (files ch01–ch03), Contents (ch04), the Introduction (ch05), then one file per era (ch06–ch10), Glossary (ch11), author note (ch12), and picture credits (ch13). The split is clean: each era file carries that era's short introduction plus a 2–4 page entry per thinker, each entry closing with a BIOGRAPHY box (name, dates, key facts, key works). Text is complete for all five eras as far as inspection shows. This is a popular illustrated survey — entries are biographical and appreciative, staging few arguments in reconstructable form — so in this KB it serves, like book 001, as orientation and a coverage checklist, not as a source for any position's canonical argument.

- **Introduction: What is Philosophy?** [file ch05]. "Philosophy begins in wonder" (Plato, as quoted here); philosophy defined as the quest to comprehend the world through reason. Branches listed: metaphysics, ontology, logic, epistemology, ethics, plus language, politics, art, science, law, religion, mind. Schools sketched in a paragraph each: sceptics, empiricists, idealists, utilitarians, logical positivists, existentialists (Sartre named the school's "founding father" — a popular simplification), analytic philosophers.
- **The Ancient World** (*c.* 7th century BC–5th century AD) [file ch06, ~9,600 words]. Era frame: Greek reason alongside the Buddha in India and Confucius and Lao-tzu in China. Thinkers: Thales of Miletus (spelled "Melitus" in the Contents), Anaximander, Siddhartha Gautama the Buddha, Confucius, Heraclitus, Parmenides, Lao-tzu, Zeno of Elea, Socrates, Democritus, Plato, Aristotle, Epicurus, Marcus Aurelius, Sextus Empiricus, Tertullian, Origen, Plotinus, St Augustine. → Connections to the epistemology entries (Sextus, scepticism) and to *Sophie's World* chs. 3–15, which cover the same ground as narrative.
- **The Middle Ages** (AD 500–1400) [file ch07, ~4,900 words]. Era frame: loss of ancient learning in the West, its preservation in the Muslim world, and the medieval project of reconciling philosophy with Christian faith — Aquinas' theology-as-science is the frame's centre. Thinkers: Avicenna, St Anselm of Canterbury, Peter Abelard, Averroes, Moses Maimonides, St Thomas Aquinas, John Duns Scotus, William of Ockham.
- **The Early Modern Era** (1400–1800) [file ch08, ~10,300 words]. Era frame: the Renaissance and the new science; the rationalists (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz) against the empiricists (Locke, Berkeley, Hume), whose principle "has influenced most philosophical thinking in the English-speaking world ever since." Thinkers: Niccolò Machiavelli, Michel de Montaigne, Sir Francis Bacon, Thomas Hobbes, René Descartes, Blaise Pascal, Baruch Spinoza, John Locke, Gottfried Leibniz, George Berkeley, Voltaire, David Hume, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Adam Smith, Immanuel Kant, Edmund Burke, Thomas Paine, Jeremy Bentham, Mary Wollstonecraft. → orientation for *External-World Skepticism* and *The Problem of Induction* (epistemology).
- **The Modern Era** (1800s–1950s) [file ch09, ~13,400 words]. Era frame: the 19th-century German "golden age," British reform and American pragmatism, then the early-20th-century reassessment of knowledge under modern science (Frege on logic, Russell on language, Heidegger on the meaning of life). Thinkers: Johann Gottlieb Fichte, Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel, James Mill, Arthur Schopenhauer, Alexis de Tocqueville, John Stuart Mill, Søren Kierkegaard, Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels, Henry Sidgwick, Charles Sanders Peirce (spelled "Pierce" in the Contents), William James, Friedrich Nietzsche, Gottlob Frege, Émile Durkheim, Edmund Husserl, John Dewey, Henri Bergson, Alfred North Whitehead, George Santayana, Max Weber, J. M. E. McTaggart, Bertrand Russell, G. E. Moore, Ludwig Wittgenstein, Martin Heidegger, Rudolf Carnap. → background for the existentialism and Nietzsche entries in this subfield.
- **The Post-Modern Era** (mid-1950s–present) [file ch10, ~13,000 words]. Era frame: postmodernism's roots pushed back to structuralism and, by some accounts, to Nietzsche and Heidegger; after Einstein, "the only certain thing was uncertainty," with Popper extending conjecture to political and social thought. (The frame dates Lyotard's first use of "post-modernism" to 1978 — a popular-level dating; treat as the book's claim, not a settled fact.) Thinkers: Max Horkheimer, Herbert Marcuse, Gilbert Ryle, Hans-Georg Gadamer, Jacques Lacan, Karl Popper, Jean-Paul Sartre, Ayn Rand, Hannah Arendt, Maurice Merleau-Ponty, Simone de Beauvoir, Willard Van Orman Quine, Claude Lévi-Strauss, Simone Weil, A. J. Ayer, Alan Turing, Donald Davidson, Peter Strawson, John Rawls, Thomas Kuhn, Paul Feyerabend, Michel Foucault, Noam Chomsky, Bernard Williams, Jacques Derrida, Robert Nozick, Saul Kripke. → background for *Postmodernism and Its Applied Turn* (this subfield), where Foucault and Derrida reappear as book 003 presents them.
- **Glossary** [file ch11]. Short definitions (Absolute, etc.) — usable as a quick orientation glossary only.

## Coverage
All five era files and the Introduction were inspected (openings read in full; thinker lists taken from the Contents file, cross-checked against the headings inside each era file). No chapter text appears missing. Defects are presentational, not structural: every file carries the same generic title, and the Contents misspells two names (noted above). The book's level is the real limitation: it reports positions, it rarely argues them, so no problem entry in this KB cites it for an argument — its role is the survey rung and the thinker-coverage checklist for the history subfield.
