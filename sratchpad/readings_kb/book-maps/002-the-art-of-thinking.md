**The book's shape.** Five long essays by Li form the Main Part (per the editors' Brief Introduction, ch02 [T]); around them sit an Introductory Part of interviews and a biographical appreciation, a Reference Part preserving the famous September 1987 debate (Li vs Michael Horner of Campus Crusade, on "Is theism more reasonable than atheism?") and the polemics it provoked, and an Appendix of two essays about Li. The methodology is stated most compactly in the two short interviews (ch07–ch08): thinking method has four departments — **semantic analysis （語理分析）, logical method, scientific method, fallacy analysis （謬誤剖析）** — of which semantic analysis is "the first link": before grounds, settle meaning. The two key questions of critical thinking: "What does it mean?" and "What are the grounds for it?" (ch08).

## Introductory Part （導引篇）

- **Brief Introduction** [ch02] [T]. Publishers' note on the definitive edition (27th printing onward): the five Main-Part essays revised page by page; new sections added; the appendix essays by Lü Xiang and Chen Qiangli added. Carries Li's aphorisms: "To lack knowledge is merely to be ignorant; to be clumsy at thinking is to be stupid"; "The only 'formula' of the art of thinking is: the art of thinking has no formula."
- **Preface** [ch03] [T]. The editors': thinking needs method; rational disputation as training for thought; the book's three parts explained.
- **Sharp Talent and Magical Charm — From Flying-Knife Kid to Philosophical Thinker** (Zhang Jingzhen) [ch05] [S]. A biographical appreciation built from press clippings: Li's reputation for razor-sharp thought and unmatched debate.
- **Wandering and Contemplation — Li Tianming Talks about His Life and Thought** (interview, Zhou Yihui) [ch06] [S]. Fourteen sections, autobiography to philosophy: semantic analysis "purified" out of analytic philosophy into the methodology's starting point (§VI); "perspective metaphysics" — metaphysical theories as viewpoints or measuring systems rather than knowledge; his "foundational philosophy": event realism (events, unlike objects, cannot be destroyed), "nine-one-ism" (the ninth-place holder has what the first-place holder lacks: his ninth), and a "religion of love." Mostly life-philosophy, not method; feeds no logic entry.
- **A Conversation with Dr Li Tianming on Methods of Thinking** [ch07] ★ [T] → *Arguments, Validity, and Soundness*; *Semantic Analysis and the Clarity of Meaning*. The four-department map of thinking methodology and each department's function (semantic analysis clarifies the problem; logical method audits whether arguments are correct; fallacy analysis classifies the common errors — personal attack, irrelevant authority, evasion — for quick identification); why thinking must be taught; independent thinking defined as command of correct method, not contrariness.
- **Dr Li Tianming on the Key to Thinking** [ch08] ★ [T] → *Semantic Analysis and the Clarity of Meaning*; *Arguments, Validity, and Soundness*. Critical thinking （批判思考， discriminating true/false, right/wrong) is the foundation, creative thinking the further step; the key habit: aptly asking "What does it mean?" and "What are the grounds for it?" — "the alertness of critical thinking."

## Main Part （主題篇） — Li's five essays

- **Methods of Thinking and Independent Thinking — Prelude to *Thinking and the Mind-Demons*** [ch09] ★ [T] → *Semantic Analysis and the Clarity of Meaning*. Part I: semantic analysis expounded — language as thinking's tool; meaningless statements are "not even false"; clarifying "democracy" before investigating; the two errors to be treated, taking obscurity for profundity and familiarity for clarity (the "from the viewpoint of…" formulas; "money or friends?"); **pseudo-semantic projection** — inferring a compound expression has meaning because its parts do (the "half a wife" example; the demolition of the "inconceivable-and-unspeakable / metaphor-and-symbol" defence of God-talk). Part II: against the "shackles thesis" (language as thought's shackle) and the "chaos thesis" ("yes is no," "change is non-change"), whose universal formula "non-x is x" Li calls the root of deluded thinking; postscript on authoritarian vs independent thinking. A footnote (note to Part II) uses Robbins's *Mathematical Logic* and Gödel numbers as an example of benign residual indeterminacy.
- **Thinking and the Mind-Demons** [ch10] ★ [T] → *Semantic Analysis and the Clarity of Meaning*; *Fallacies and Sophistry*; *Closed Systems and Pseudo-Explanation*. The book's methodological core: a practical framework for the critique of **linguistic harms （語害）** in three categories. **I. Semantic ambiguity**: "mission" and "fate"; dialectical "negation"; terminological camouflage (numerical and arts-critical); semantic floating （虛浮 — vague words without fixed standards, degrees allowed: "young" has approximate bounds, "counter-revolutionary" has none) and semantic incoherence （錯亂 — wholly meaningless word-salad). **II. Empty verbiage**: tautologies (necessarily true, zero information content) versus empirical truths; **empty propositions** — tautologies masquerading as factual statements; empty talk used as grounds ("what will happen will happen"); concealed emptiness ("language is just language"; reflection theory's "distorted reflection" = "reflects or does not reflect"); **forced definitions that manufacture emptiness （強定成空）** — defining "human" to include innate goodness, the true-Chinese-mother / no-true-Scotsman pattern. **III. Conceptual slippage**: conceptual confusion (equivocation between licit senses: "same" as identical vs similar — the verbal-dispute engine; the three-birds examination question, mathematics vs experience) and conceptual distortion (senses that violate usage: criticising religion as "infringing religious freedom"; utilitarianism misread; **calling a deer a horse** — "another kind of freedom," "real equality," "highest-grade democracy"; Voltaire's Holy Roman Empire). **Conclusion: closed systems** — all three harms share irrefutability; the divine-will theory and the class-imprint theory as pseudo-explanations (hindsight only, no prediction); the "truth-illusion" of self-appointed messengers of truth.
- **Critical Thinking and Religion — Prelude to *From the Journey…*** [ch11] ★ [R — read in the original; translation outstanding]. Part I: point-by-point refutation of Yang Dongchuan's critique of the September Debate (fairness; "a feint?"; **God and the stone**; **is God beyond logic?**). Part II: sophistry vs critical thinking — the "Triangle Argument" dissected (three blind spots: conceptual incoherence, inverted logic, muddled inference); then **the way of critical disputation**: the two senses of "winning" (being right vs being awarded the win); how the refuted react (silence; doubled sophistry); the **three knacks of sophistry** — seem-plausible, make-up-in-volume, move-by-emotion.
- **From the Journey of the Bloody Road to the Journey to the Kingdom of Heaven** [ch12] ★ [R in part — prologue, taxonomy sections, and headings read in the original; translation outstanding] → *Fallacies and Sophistry*. The construction of an **anti-sophistry system**, worked over Leung Yancheng's 20,000-character *Review*: the **four blindnesses** （喻盲 metaphor-, 諷盲 irony-, 語理盲 semantic-analysis-, 邏輯盲 logic-blindness), the **three delusions** （標準妄 the criterion delusion — demanding criteria before criticism, which self-traps; 斷章妄 the out-of-context delusion; 不相干妄 the irrelevance delusion), the **five tricks** （東拉西扯 dragging in irrelevancies; 無中生有 fabricating from nothing; 魚目混珠 passing fish eyes off as pearls; 訴諸詆詬 resorting to abuse; 退縮逃溜 shrinking and fleeing) — twelve "fatal points" in all.
- **Essays in Critical Thinking — Variations on *From the Journey…*** [ch13] ★ [T] → *Fallacies and Sophistry*; *Closed Systems and Pseudo-Explanation*; *Gödelian Self-Reference and the Limits of Formal Systems* (Barber section). Worked examples against Lam Shui-kei's *Surprised* and Cheng Chi-shing's *Religion*: pseudo-refutation and pseudo-proof; **begging the question** ("God loves the world" presupposes God's existence); the strict definition of self-contradiction. §II fixes the taxonomy **fallacy （謬誤， error of thinking method) vs factual error （訛誤， error of knowledge)** and catalogues: appeal to ignorance, the self-defeating fallacy, the **fallacy of false-value transmission** (from "premises false" inferring "conclusion false"), non-sequitur; factual errors about the syllogism (syllogism is a tiny corner of first-order logic; infinitely many valid arguments are not syllogisms — "all circles are figures, so whoever draws a circle draws a figure") and about contradictory vs contrary propositions. §III: seeming explanations (no verifiability, no predictive power — "hindsight-cannon explanations"); "specifying" a term is not clarifying it. §IV: the **anti-omnipotence argument** (the stone dilemma), its erroneous replies, and the **Barber of Seville** — self-referential concepts that harbour hidden contradictions (Russell's paradox, the set Ω) — ending with the demand for a **consistency proof** and Li's proposed replacement concept, maximal power （至能）. Conclusion: the logic of thinking (A semantic analysis, B fallacy analysis, C deductive logic, D scientific norms) vs the science of thinking (E creative strategy, bracketed as dubious).

## Reference Part （參考篇）

- **God Does Not Exist?!** [ch14] [S]. The September Debate transcript itself (Horner's six "best explanation" arguments; Li's counters, including "omnipotence harbours a hidden contradiction" and "do not confuse reason with faith"), compiled by the Hong Kong Campus Crusade. The object of study for ch11–ch13, not an exposition of method.
- **"You Are Not a Fish — How Do You Know the Fish Are Happy?"** (Yang Dongchuan) [ch15] [S]. The critique of the debate (unfair judging; shadow-boxing) that ch11 Part I answers.
- **A Review of Li Tianming's "Critical Thinking and Religion"** (Leung Yancheng) [ch16] [S]. The long counter-attack dissected in ch12.
- **An Apology to Li Tianming** (Leung Yancheng) [ch17] [S]. Leung's published apology, withdrawing most of the *Review* and keeping only its stone-problem section.
- **Surprised that Li Tianming Failed to Hit the Mark with a Single Arrow** (Lam Shui-kei) [ch18] [S]. The *Surprised* article analysed in ch13 §I.
- **Religion and Critical Reflection** (Cheng Chi-shing) [ch19] [S]. The *Religion* book analysed in ch13 §§II–IV.
- **Starting from the Article "A Review of…"** (Shen Zhihe) [ch20] [S]. A third-party defence of Li: exposes Leung's face-value readings of "blind argument" etc., and names the ad hominem pattern directly.

## Appendix （附錄）

- **The Tyranny of Language and the Art of Thinking** (Lü Xiang) and **On Li Tianming's Method of Thinking** (Chen Qiangli) [ch21] [S — structure]. Secondary literature on Li: the move "from semantic analysis to the critique of linguistic harms," and independent thinking "from method to art." No new method content drawn on.
