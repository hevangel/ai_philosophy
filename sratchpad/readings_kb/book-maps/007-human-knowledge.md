# Book map — 007: Paul K. Moser & Arnold vander Nat (eds.), *Human Knowledge: Classical and Contemporary Approaches* (3rd ed., OUP, 2003)

*Book layer · one of the pilot's two source textbooks. ★ = chapter feeds one of the three pilot problem entries. Status tags describe the corpus file, not the reading itself.*

**Extraction note — read this first.** Book 007 is *not* an extraction of the book. No epub was found on the mirror, so the folder (`data/philosophy_readings/markdown/007 - Human Knowledge…/`) is a **reconstruction** (its `_meta.json` says so, provenance "reconstructed", built 2026-10-04): for each of the anthology's 39 readings there is one file, containing (a) a full public-domain or openly hosted text where one could be fetched, (b) a *citation stub* — title, citation, and best access point — where it could not, or (c) in three cases, a fetch failure or corrupted file. Several "selections" are in fact the complete source works (the anthology's excerpts are shorter). The editors' General Introduction has no file. `toc.md` in the folder is a sourced reconstruction of the 3rd-edition table of contents. Statuses below were verified file by file.

## Part I — Classical sources

### Greek and Medieval
- **1. Plato — *Meno; Phaedo; Republic; Theaetetus*** [full text]. Gutenberg Jowett translations (the file is the *Meno* with the other dialogues' selections). Knowledge and recollection; the *Theaetetus* on knowledge vs true belief — the prehistory of the analysis-of-knowledge problem.
- **2. Aristotle — *Posterior Analytics; De Anima*** [full text, oversized]. The file is the Internet Archive text of the whole Oxford *Works of Aristotle* vol. I, much wider than the assigned selections. Demonstration, first principles, and the soul's cognitive powers.
- **3. Sextus Empiricus — *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*** [stub] ★. The Pyrrhonian modes — the classical source of the skepticism problem. Citation only (Bury's Loeb translation remains in copyright); no usable text.
- **4. Augustine — *Contra Academicos; De Civitas Dei*** [partial]. New Advent (NPNF) text of *City of God* selections, ~1,700 words usable. Augustine against Academic scepticism; knowledge and divine illumination.
- **5. Thomas Aquinas — *Summa Theologiae* (selections)** [partial]. New Advent text, ~460 words including site boilerplate. Truth and the intellect.

### Early Modern
- **6. René Descartes — *Meditations on First Philosophy*** [FAILED] ★. The 4 KB file contains only Wikisource navigation boilerplate — the Veitch translation text was never captured. The pilot's cogito material therefore comes from Russell (book 006, ch. II) and the Cogito lectures, not from this file.
- **7. John Locke — *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*** [full text, oversized]. The complete Gutenberg *Essay*, presented as selections. Ideas, degrees of knowledge, probability — the empiricist frame for the whole subfield.
- **8. Gottfried Leibniz — Introduction to *New Essays on the Human Understanding*** [full text, oversized]. The complete Langley translation. Leibniz's reply to Locke: innate truths and the a priori.
- **9. George Berkeley — *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*** [full text] ★ (supports *External-World Skepticism*). *Esse est percipi*; the primary text behind Russell's ch. I discussion of idealism.
- **10. David Hume — *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*** [full text, incl. the edition's index] ★. The pilot's primary source twice over: §§IV–V state the problem of induction and its sceptical solution; §XII carries the external-world argument and the doctrine of mitigated scepticism.
- **11. Thomas Reid — *Inquiry into the Human Mind*** [full text]. Selections from a 19th-century edition scan. Common-sense first principles against the "ideal theory" — Reid's anti-skeptical stand.
- **12. Immanuel Kant — *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics*** [full text]. The synthetic a priori; Kant's answer to Hume. Feeds *The Problem of Induction*'s sequel (a priori knowledge).

## Part II — Contemporary sources

### Pragmatism and Empiricism
- **13. William James — "The Will to Believe"** [full text, oversized]. The file is Gutenberg's complete *The Will to Believe and Other Essays* collection, not the single essay. When believing beyond the evidence is permissible.
- **14. Bertrand Russell — "Appearance, Reality, and Knowledge by Acquaintance"** [full text] ★. The file is the complete Gutenberg *Problems of Philosophy* (all fifteen chapters) under the excerpt's title — i.e., in this corpus book 007 ch14 duplicates book 006 entire. Source of the pilot's Russell material in anthology placement.
- **15. A. J. Ayer — "Verification and Philosophy"** [stub]. The verification principle and its critics.
- **16. C. I. Lewis — "The Pragmatic Element in Knowledge"** [stub]. The given and its interpretation.
- **17. Rudolf Carnap — "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology"** [stub]. Internal vs external questions.
- **18. W. V. Quine — "Two Dogmas of Empiricism"** [CORRUPTED]. Header intact; the body is binary garbage (a failed ditext.com fetch, same failure mode as ch20). One of the century's most anthologized epistemology papers is unreadable here.
- **19. Richard Rorty — "Pragmatism, Relativism, and Irrationalism"** [stub]. Knowledge as conversation rather than mirroring.

### The Analysis of Knowledge
- **20. Edmund Gettier — "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?"** [CORRUPTED] ★. The pilot's primary text — and unreadable: title page and source line survive, the body is ~2.5 KB of binary garbage. The pilot's Gettier entry is therefore built on the Carneades lecture's presentation, and says so.
- **21. Richard Feldman — "An Alleged Defect in Gettier Counter-Examples"** [stub] ★. The anthology's first response to Gettier; citation only.
- **22. John Pollock — "The Gettier Problem"** [stub] ★. A systematic treatment (ch. 3 of *Knowledge and Justification*, 1974); citation only.

## Coverage summary
Usable full or partial text: chs. 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 (12 of 39). Citation stubs: 25 chapters. Corrupted: ch18, ch20. Failed fetch: ch06. Missing entirely: the editors' General Introduction. For the pilot, the heavy lifting is done by ch10 (Hume) and ch14 (Russell); the anthology's contemporary core — including its entire Analysis of Knowledge and Skepticism sections — is present only as a table of contents with citations.
