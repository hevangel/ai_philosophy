# Philosophy and Popular Culture — Master Bibliography

This document consolidates the bibliography built in our conversation.

## Scope and counting notes

This is a practical master bibliography of English-language books explicitly marketed as philosophy-through-popular-culture or as philosophical studies of a particular popular-cultural subject.

The numbering below is our own continuous master numbering, not the publishers' official series numbering, except where an official volume number is explicitly shown.

A few caveats:

- Some publishers have changed imprints over time.
- Some books were revised, expanded, or superseded by later “Ultimate” editions; where they are distinct catalogue entries, they are listed separately.
- Some successor series restarted their official numbering.
- A few titles are forthcoming rather than already published.
- Once we leave official branded series and include independent scholarship, the boundary becomes fuzzy. This should be understood as a broad working corpus rather than a mathematically final list of every philosophy/pop-culture book ever written.

# Historical note

**The Metaphysics of Star Trek** appeared in 1997, before *Seinfeld and Philosophy*. Open Court therefore did not invent the idea of philosophizing popular culture; its major contribution was to turn the practice into a recognizable, repeatable publishing genre with a long-running branded series.

# Working definition of the corpus

A book belongs in this document if it falls into at least one of these categories:

1. It belongs to an official philosophy-and-popular-culture series.
2. It explicitly uses a popular-cultural franchise, artist, genre, sport, game, technology, or cultural practice as the organizing subject for philosophical analysis.
3. It is marketed as an accessible bridge between philosophy and popular culture.
4. It is a field-level study of philosophy's engagement with popular culture.

This definition deliberately excludes ordinary academic books that merely happen to discuss film, literature, music, games, or culture without presenting themselves as part of the philosophy/pop-culture genre.
