## Bibliographic note (important)

The title on the owner's list — "Aesthetics: An Introduction to the
Philosophy of Art" — does not match any Dabney Townsend book. That exact
title belongs to **Anne Sheppard** (OUP Opus, 1987). Townsend's 1997
introductory book — almost certainly the one the owner read — is:

Dabney Townsend, *An Introduction to Aesthetics*, Blackwell Publishers
(Introducing Philosophy series, no. 5), 1997, vi + 248 pp.,
ISBN 1-55786-730-5 pb / 1-55786-731-3.

The download list (books.json #15) should search "An Introduction to
Aesthetics" for the PDF retry.

## What the chapters cover (only sourced descriptions)

- **Overall (Wiley publisher description):** "a fascinating introduction to
  the core themes and basic methods of aesthetics and the philosophy of
  art", an "analytic and historical treatment of the issues" with "many
  illustrative examples", aimed at "the philosophical novice"; appendix of
  referenced texts, glossary, further-reading suggestions.
- **Ch. 1** (weakly sourced review snippet): how we treat art differently
  from non-art; the chapter's own subsections indicate coverage of aesthetic
  language, criticism and value terms, and the problem of definition.
- **Chs. 2–4, general character** (Peter Lamarque's review essay): Townsend
  runs through the classic theories of art — imitation, expression,
  significant form, functionalism, institutionalism — "with characteristic
  scrupulousness"; examines "what kinds of objects artworks are"
  (intentional objects, depiction, ontology, fictionality); explains the
  epistemology of Croce's expression theory more fully than rival
  introductions. Lamarque notes Townsend labels his own preferred outlook
  "postmodern" — the art world as creating the possibility of aesthetic
  production — and that Arthur Danto goes undiscussed. Verdict: the most
  solid of the 1990s introductions on the arguments for and against standard
  positions.
- No source was found for sentence-level descriptions of chapters 3, 4, 5
  individually; they are not invented here.
