## What the book covers (sourced)

- OUP description (3rd ed.): "a unique and wide-ranging examination of the
  theory of knowledge", blending classical readings (Plato, Aristotle, Sextus
  Empiricus, Augustine, Aquinas, Descartes, Locke, Leibniz, Berkeley, Hume,
  Reid, Kant) with contemporary analytic epistemology (James, Russell, Ayer,
  Lewis, Carnap, Quine, Rorty, Chisholm, Kripke, Moore, Wittgenstein,
  Strawson, Putnam, and the Gettier/justification/skepticism/naturalized-
  epistemology debates). Organized chronologically and thematically, with the
  editors' general introduction, section overviews, and bibliographies.
- The numbered chapters (1–39) sit under six thematic section headings: under
  Part I, "Greek and Medieval Sources" and "Early Modern Sources"; under
  Part II, "Pragmatism and Empiricism", "The Analysis of Knowledge",
  "A Priori Knowledge", "Justified Belief", "Skepticism", and
  "Epistemology and Psychology".
- No source was found describing individual chapters one by one; the
  selection titles above are the verified granularity.
