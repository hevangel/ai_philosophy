## What the chapters cover (only sourced descriptions)

- **Overall structure (Kymlicka's own preface, OCR):** the book frames
  political theories in two groups — "mainstream" defences of liberal
  democracy (utilitarianism, liberal equality, libertarianism) and
  critiques/alternatives to it (Marxism, communitarianism, feminism, civic
  republicanism, multiculturalism) — organized as a narrative in which each
  theory responds to weaknesses in earlier ones.
- **1. Utilitarianism** (chapter opening, OCR): the background theory Rawls
  responded to; its "two attractions" (secularism and consequentialism);
  competing definitions of utility; problems with the maximizing instruction,
  including special obligations and "illegitimate preferences".
- **2. Liberal Equality** (review snippet only, Soifer 1992 — weakly
  sourced): covers Rawls and Dworkin.
- **3. Libertarianism** (same snippet — weakly sourced): covers Nozick.
- **4. Marxism** (preface, OCR): the critique of "the exploitation and
  alienation of wage-labourers"; discusses Roemer and G. A. Cohen (Amazon).
- **5. Communitarianism** (preface/introduction, OCR): criticizes "social
  atomism" and ahistorical standards of political judgement; discusses
  Sandel, Taylor, Walzer, Miller (Amazon).
- **6. Citizenship Theory** (preface, OCR): one of two new chapters in this
  edition; "citizenship" as the buzzword of the 1990s (as "justice" was in
  the 1970s and "community" in the 1980s); civic skills, virtues, education,
  public reason, deliberative democracy.
- **7. Multiculturalism** (preface + publisher, OCR/Amazon): the second new
  chapter; citizenship and group difference — politics of recognition,
  individual vs. group rights, nationalism, racism, immigration, group
  representation.
- **8. Feminism** (preface + introduction, OCR): the critique of "the
  subordination of women", including the "ethic of care" criticism; discusses
  Gilligan, MacKinnon, Okin (Amazon).
- **General (publisher, Amazon):** Kymlicka's "best selling critical
  introduction to contemporary political theory", fully revised from the
  1st ed. to incorporate developments of the previous 11 years; two new
  chapters on citizenship theory and multiculturalism; extended
  further-reading guides per chapter.
